Yemen’s government forces have pulled back from Bab el-Mandeb, so both of the Gulf’s sea exits are now contested, and pump prices are still falling

Geopolitics → Energy Continuing thread Independent corroboration

On Sunday, Yemen’s Saudi-backed Southern Armed Forces said they were redeploying out of Dhubab airport and the Bab el-Mandeb area west of Taiz. That leaves the Iran-aligned Houthis in control of the ground above the strait. Government troops had retaken Dhubab and the port of Mokha only last month. The southern forces say the move is about defending Aden and breaking the Houthi siege of Taiz. Reuters and The National both describe it as handing the Houthis leverage over the waterway.

This is one chain, not two stories. Hormuz has been effectively closed since the war began in February. That made Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea outlet at Yanbu its main way around the strait. The National reports that Houthi strikes on the kingdom have already hit ports, airports and Aramco sites, Yanbu among them. Ships leaving Yanbu for Asia or the Indian Ocean pass Bab el-Mandeb.

At the other exit, Iran’s navy and Revolutionary Guards said three more tankers hit mines on Saturday while running dark on routes Tehran has not authorised. No ships were named, and nobody independent has confirmed it. On Sunday the Guards said six drones hit a US radar at Bahrain’s Riffa Air Base. It was the second claimed attack on Bahrain in three days.

At the pump, AAA has regular at $3.824 this morning, down about 20 cents in a week. Diesel is at $6.020, down from $6.193. The EIA weekly diesel series has fallen two weeks running, from $6.352 to $6.127.

What to watch: whether shipping insurers reprice Red Sea transits this week, a CENTCOM statement on Sunday’s Bahrain claim, and Tuesday’s EIA weekly print (17 November), which is the first that can show whether diesel has really broken below $6.

Divergence — did Iran hit anything?

What is disputed (facts). Iran says its mines set three tankers on fire on Saturday and that it destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain on Friday. CENTCOM says it shot down two drones on Friday, with no injuries and no damage. It has not commented on Sunday. The US also says it has cleared the main shipping lanes of mines.

Why they differ. The plainest reason is access. Neither side lets anyone verify its claims, and both have reasons to tell it their way: Tehran wants ship-owners to believe its permit system is enforced, and Washington wants them to believe its escorts work. Timing matters too. Iran announces within hours, and the US usually answers a day or more later.

What it means for you. Most of the evidence is on the US side. UKMTO, the British navy’s shipping agency and the one neutral counter, logged at least seven tanker attacks in the week to 11 November and put traffic about 65 per cent below prewar levels. So attacks are real, but not every claimed hit is. Assume Iran’s individual claims are inflated and the pattern behind them is real. What would settle it is a named ship in a UKMTO notice or a Lloyd’s casualty report.

Divergence — is diesel under $6?

What is disputed (facts). Reuters says US diesel fell below $6, to $5.998, a two-month low. AAA’s national average this morning is $6.020.

Why they differ. They are different price surveys, and nothing more. Reuters’ syndicated copy does not say which series it used. AAA is the daily card average this brief uses for levels.

What it means for you. Both series agree on the direction, and so does the EIA weekly: diesel is falling. Whether it is under $6 depends on which survey you read. On the instruments this brief uses, it is not yet. If the EIA weekly prints below $6.00 on Tuesday, it is.

Commerce is drafting a narrow rule on renting US AI chips to Chinese firms abroad, and Lutnick’s old firm has clients it would touch

AI & tech industry Continuing thread Single-sourced

Chinese firms cannot buy Nvidia’s best chips, but they can rent them in data centres in Malaysia, Thailand or Indonesia. The Biden administration’s global rule on that remote access was revoked early last year, and the replacement is still being written. According to the New York Times on Sunday, Howard Lutnick’s Commerce Department has pushed for a much narrower version than other parts of the administration want.

Under the Commerce draft, as described to the Times by people familiar with it, the rule would cover only seven countries: Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Brazil, South Africa and Norway. It would apply to future shipments only, so data centres already full of US chips would be exempt. Operators would have to notify the government when Chinese customers train models on their hardware, but they would not automatically need a licence, and the compute threshold would be set higher. The Information reported the proposal first.

The Times also documents overlap with Cantor Fitzgerald, which Lutnick ran for decades and his sons now run. Cantor was a sales agent this year on an equity offering of up to $1 billion by Bitdeer. Bitdeer’s Johor data centre, called A201, opens next year and is likely to serve Tencent. Cantor also has a stake in Bitdeer through its investment in Tether. Commerce says Lutnick has met every ethics obligation. The Times says it found no evidence that Cantor’s holdings shaped his decisions. On Sunday, Megaspeed told CNBC that Alice Huang left the company on 30 August 2025, and said it has hired outside lawyers for an internal investigation.

What to watch: the rule text when it goes to the White House budget office for review, and Lutnick’s testimony on 10 December.

Divergence — conflict, or trade policy?

What is disputed (significance). The facts about Cantor’s clients are documented, and Commerce does not dispute them. The argument is over what explains the narrow scope. The Times sets it beside the family business. Commerce says Lutnick decides in the national interest. Lutnick has long argued that tight controls cost Nvidia sales and strain trade relations.

Why they differ. Interest explains part of it: Commerce is defending its secretary. Access explains the rest. The Times’ account of the draft comes from people inside the dispute, and those people are mostly on the side that wants a global rule.

What it means for you. Don’t read the scope as proof of a conflict. A seven-country rule is also what a Commerce secretary would write if he cared most about trade relations, and his record fits that. Judge the rule on its effect. CFR’s Chris McGuire says China “will just rent from the data centers outside those countries.” Two things would settle it: whether the final text covers the whole world, and whether it covers chips already installed.

Six top Chinese AI models carry the fingerprint of Nvidia’s export-banned Blackwell chips, and an independent replication has confirmed part of it

AI & tech industry New subject Independent corroboration

Will DePue, a former OpenAI engineer who now runs a consultancy called Unemployment Labs, has published a way to tell which GPU trained a model just by looking at its published weights. When a model is set up, its starting weights are spread across the chip’s processing units in a pattern that depends on the chip’s layout. Some of that pattern survives training.

DePue looked at eleven models. He says six, from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group, show the pattern of Blackwell, the Nvidia generation that cannot be sold to China.

The Institute for Progress has since rerun part of the study on real hardware. It confirmed the starting patterns for three of the eleven models: DeepSeek-V3-Base on an H200, and DeepSeek-V4-Flash and MiniMax-M3 on a B200. In V4-Flash’s trained weights it found the Blackwell signal at r=0.065, against 0.0033 on Hopper. The study had reported 0.066 and 0.001. IFP could not test the other eight. Seven had no public samples, and one needed a chip it did not have.

Separately, the security firm Lava says it found 32 Blackwell Ultra B300 GPUs on exposed monitoring endpoints with IP addresses that geolocate to China.

This is evidence about the chips themselves, not about Nvidia’s boardroom. It means the export controls are failing at the training run.

Divergence — proof of smuggling, or proof of access?

What is disputed (significance). Some headlines say export-banned chips are behind China’s top models. The study’s own authors say less: the method shows which hardware was used, not where it was, how it was reached, or whether any law was broken.

Why they differ. Headline writers push it further than the evidence goes. The authors are more careful because they know what the statistics can show. IFP checked only the part it could replicate.

What it means for you. Believe the narrow claim. It has been independently confirmed for one DeepSeek model and one MiniMax model. Don’t assume the chips were smuggled. Renting Blackwells in a Malaysian data centre is legal today, and that is exactly the loophole in the story above. So this finding is the strongest argument yet for a global, retroactive remote-access rule. If the IFP replication is extended to the Kimi and Ant models, the six-model claim is settled.

The new US–China trade council met for the first time on Friday, and the tariff-cut package still is not finished four days before Trump meets Xi

World economy · trade Continuing thread Primary source

The legal half. The two governments agreed in May to set up a trade council. Its first meeting was held by video on Friday, and China’s Commerce Ministry put out its account on Saturday. It covered tariffs, agricultural trade, and the reciprocal tariff cut agreed at the eighth round of talks in New York and Washington on 20–23 September. That deal would lower tariffs on about $30 billion of each side’s goods, and bring about 90 per cent of the products covered down to most-favoured-nation rates. The ministry called the talks candid and constructive. It did not say anything had been signed. Trump meets Xi on Thursday at APEC in Shenzhen.

The market half. Beijing keeps saying the tariff cut has to take effect before it buys more US farm goods. Chinese purchases of US farm goods other than soybeans are on track for a seven-year low, according to a Reuters analysis of customs data. That is despite a pledge to buy $17 billion a year through 2028. For US growers the issue is timing: a deal signed Thursday would come too late for the bulk of this year’s export season.

Divergence — a $30 billion deal or a $60 billion one?

What is disputed (facts, apparently). China’s official English summary says about $30 billion. CNBC and Reuters say $60 billion.

Why they differ. They are counting differently. The ministry’s own phrase, quoted by CNBC, is a “$30 billion-for-$30 billion” arrangement. Thirty billion each way is sixty in total.

What it means for you. It is the same package. Use $30 billion each way. Anything larger announced Thursday would be new.

Zelensky says US negotiators asked, for the first time, whether Ukraine would leave Donbas in exchange for joining NATO

Geopolitics Continuing thread Single-sourced

Zelensky told reporters on Friday, in remarks his office released Saturday, that US negotiators asked whether Ukraine would leave Donbas if it could join NATO. He said it was the first time the question had been raised. This came after Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met him and Putin separately on Thursday, following two days of talks in Abu Dhabi that produced no breakthrough.

He said there is no concrete proposal yet. If one comes with NATO and EU guarantees, a properly armed Ukrainian army and funded reconstruction, he would put it to the public. He said Ukraine would never recognise the occupied land as Russian.

Here is the US channel. By Zelensky’s account, Washington describes what it would offer as bilateral guarantees like Israel’s, “similar to” NATO’s Articles 4 and 5, with no US troops in Ukraine. That would be a new long-term US defence commitment, and Congress would have to fund it.

A US official told the BBC that “many ideas continue to be discussed.” Kushner asked for confidentiality and warned against “maximalist” reported proposals. He also said the US now wants to help Russia and Europe talk to each other directly.

Divergence — an offer, or a question?

What is disputed (facts). Zelensky describes a US question that put NATO membership “back on the agenda.” Washington has not confirmed it. Kushner’s comments point the other way.

Why they differ. Each side has a reason. Zelensky gains by locking in NATO as the reference point before any deal. Kushner gains by keeping his options open with Moscow, which has always treated NATO membership as unacceptable. Only one side has spoken on the record, and everyone else is reporting what Zelensky said.

What it means for you. Treat it as a question US negotiators really did raise, not as an offer. The language Zelensky reports, “something similar” to NATO with no US troops, is weaker than membership, and that is the bit to remember. Watch for a US written text or a NATO statement. Either would settle it, and so would a Russian answer.

OpenAI’s agents can now spend on Visa, Mastercard and Amex, and JPMorgan has signed up for its coding agents

US financials · AI New subject Independent corroboration

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday. It lets the company’s “dots” agents buy things for users. For now it is open only to US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 or over. OpenAI built it with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, and The Verge reports that Chase cards also work.

The design is conservative. The agent never sees the card number. For each purchase the network issues a one-time token, as Apple Pay does, and the user approves every checkout with a fingerprint or face scan. OpenAI says it is not taking a cut of transactions yet. It says users will eventually be able to hand purchases over entirely, within spending limits.

The same week, OpenAI announced a contract under which JPMorganChase will use its Codex agents to write, review and ship code, and use its models for cybersecurity. American Banker reports that the bank is keeping several AI vendors and did not disclose terms.

Why this belongs in a financials brief: agent shopping is starting on the card networks, not on stablecoins. That keeps issuers’ rewards, fraud rules and dispute rights in place, which protects interchange. The open questions are liability and access. Who pays when an agent buys the wrong thing, and will merchants let agents in at all? Amazon has already blocked Meta’s Muse agents.

HHS has scrapped the rule requiring Head Start to pay teachers like public pre-K, and says that saves more than $2 billion

US politics & policy New subject Primary source

A final rule from the Administration for Children and Families goes into the Federal Register on Monday. It repeals the wage and benefit requirements of the 2024 Head Start rule. Those required programs with more than 200 slots to raise pay by 2031 to match local public pre-K teachers, or 90 per cent of kindergarten teacher pay where a district has no public pre-K. Smaller programs only had to show progress.

ACF puts the savings to programs at more than $2 billion. The rule takes effect 60 days after publication. Programs can still raise pay if they choose. ACF says most have not yet done much to comply, since the deadline was years away.

The fiscal point is that the 2024 mandate came with no money. Head Start costs about $12 billion a year and has roughly 700,000 slots. When HHS wrote the 2024 rule, it admitted that without more funding the pay rise could mean about 110,000 fewer children served. It included a waiver in case Congress did not raise funding by more than 1.3 per cent by 2028.

Separately, a broader proposal to remove Head Start quality standards is still pending. It covers staff-to-child ratios, health screenings and family services.

Divergence — saving slots, or losing teachers?

What is disputed (significance). The administration says it is removing an unfunded mandate that would have forced layoffs. Supporters of the 2024 rule say pay was the cause of the turnover. In October 2023, 15 per cent of Head Start staff positions were empty, and empty positions leave slots unfilled.

Why they differ. Both sides use the same numbers. They disagree about what happens next. If Congress never funds the raise, the mandate costs slots, and the administration’s reading wins. If pay stays low, turnover costs slots too.

What it means for you. The repeal settles the mandate, not the problem. Watch the FY2027 Head Start appropriation. With Democrats taking Congress in January, the money the 2024 rule lacked could come as a line item instead. Vacancy rates over the next two years will show which reading was right.

Florida: the attorney general has stopped defending the state’s 2018 bump-stock ban, but the law is still on the books

Florida & Tampa Bay New subject Independent corroboration

Attorney General James Uthmeier said on Friday that his office will no longer defend convictions under Florida’s bump-stock ban when they are appealed. He issued a formal legal opinion calling the 2018 law unconstitutional. The opinion says it bans “an entire class of firearm components,” and that the Second Amendment protects semi-automatic firearms modified that way. The ban was part of the gun package passed after the Parkland school shooting, where 17 people were killed. Breaking it carries up to five years in prison.

A point of law the coverage blurs: the Supreme Court’s 2024 decision that struck down the federal bump-stock ban turned on a statute, not the Constitution. The Court ruled 6–3 that a bump stock does not turn a rifle into a machine gun under federal law. It did not decide whether bump stocks are constitutionally protected. Uthmeier’s argument goes further than the Court did.

Divergence — is the ban dead?

What is disputed (significance). WUSF’s headline says Uthmeier “declares bump stock ban dead.” CBS Miami points out that the statute has not been repealed.

Why they differ. The headline describes the AG’s intent. The other account describes the law. An attorney general’s opinion is advisory. It does not bind trial courts, and it does not stop elected state attorneys, including the one covering Pasco and Pinellas, from bringing charges.

What it means for you. In Pasco, owning a bump stock is still a felony. What has changed is that a conviction is now much more likely to be overturned on appeal, because the state will not defend it. What would settle it: a Florida appeals court ruling on the Second Amendment question, or the Legislature repealing the law.
