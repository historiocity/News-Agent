Refining is now a defence shortfall, and the conditions on paying for it have been waived

Energy & grid US debt & fiscal Continuation Primary document Underreported

This brief has spent six weeks on the price of diesel without being able to say what anyone intended to do about it. Presidential Determination No. 2027-01, signed 22 October and published this morning, is the answer. It invokes section 303 of the Defense Production Act and it is addressed not to the Secretary of Energy but to the Secretary of War.

The determination finds that domestic petroleum production, refining and logistics capacity — "including exploration and production, gathering and transmission pipelines, storage, and marine terminals" — are "industrial resources, materials, or critical technology items essential to the national defense". It finds that industry "cannot reasonably be expected to provide these capabilities… in a timely manner due to constrained financing, long lead times, permitting and infrastructure bottlenecks, and supply chain limitations". And it finds that purchases, purchase commitments and "financial support for the development of production capabilities" are the most practical remedy.

Then comes the sentence that matters most. Having declared a national emergency in Executive Order 14156 in January 2025, the President determines that action is needed "to avert an industrial resource or critical technology item shortfall that would severely impair national defense capability" and, "pursuant to section 303(a)(7) of the Act, I waive the requirements of section 303(a)(1)-(a)(6)". Those paragraphs are the conditions Congress attached to Title III spending. The Secretary of War is "authorized and directed… including making necessary purchases, commitments, and financial instruments to enable these projects".

Title III is the Defense Production Act's money: the authority to buy output, guarantee purchases, and put federal capital directly into private plant. Used here, it means the government may fund refinery and pipeline capacity as defence procurement, outside the gating Congress wrote. Whether that is a sensible response to a genuine constraint or a very large discretionary spending authority created by memorandum is the question, and the document does not name a dollar figure, a project, or an end date.

What to watch: the first award and who receives it, which converts this from a finding into a programme; whether any congressional committee asks about the (a)(7) waiver; and whether refinery utilisation, discussed in the next story, moves at all on a timescale shorter than years.

A cruise ship has been ended by its fuel bill, and the refineries explain why

Energy & grid Macro & markets Continuation Independent corroboration

The Odyssey set sail two years ago on a three-year residential world voyage, sold as living at sea while seeing seven continents. It is tied up in Sihanoukville, Cambodia, where it docked on 23 October for a five-day stop and has not left. Villa Vie Residences has suspended operations and told its passengers to go home, which for them means leaving the home they bought.

The company gave the arithmetic. Monthly fuel cost per cabin, historically around $1,800, has reached an estimated $6,000 to $8,000 in parts of Asia during the war in Iran. It slowed the itinerary and spent longer in port to burn less; it was not enough. "We have been unable to continue carrying the resulting operating losses… Passing the full increase on to residents is not a realistic solution." There were 203 passengers aboard, mostly American, across fifteen nationalities, and 156 crew, on a ship built for 650. The itinerary was confirmed into November 2027. The crew's position is unclear.

On the supply side this brief can now say more than it could. US refinery utilisation ran at 98.0 per cent in late August, fell to 92.5 by 25 September, and has recovered only to 93.4 per cent in the week to 16 October. Distillate stocks over the same period went 104.2 million barrels, up to 107.9, back down to 102.6, and up again to 104.3. Stocks have gone nowhere; the refineries making the fuel have been running four to five points below their August rate for two months. That is a production story rather than an inventory story, and it fits what the prices are doing better than the mechanism this brief withdrew on 22 October.

The prices today: diesel $6.3499, down 0.22 cents; regular $4.2471, down 1.90. Both fell, and the gap widened anyway, to $2.1028 — above $2.10 for the first time, and a fourth consecutive record.

What to watch: this week's distillate and utilisation figures, which land at 10:30 this morning and are discussed below the line; whether utilisation climbs back toward 98 per cent; and whether any Title III money from the previous story is aimed at throughput rather than new capacity.

Brazil told Flávio Bolsonaro not to leave; the United States is issuing him a visa

Foreign policy Courts Continuation Independent corroboration

On Monday Brazil's Supreme Court ordered Senator Flávio Bolsonaro to surrender his passports within 24 hours and barred him from leaving the country. He filmed a video saying federal police had stopped him outside his father's house, that he would comply, and that "I never thought about leaving the country. I will hand over my passports and defend myself against this aberration." On Tuesday Brazilian media reported he had left for the United States. His campaign manager, his lawyer and his political allies say they do not know where he is.

Also on Tuesday, a senior US administration official said the State Department will issue visas to Bolsonaro and his family. That is the United States announcing it will admit a man whose own country's highest court has just forbidden to travel, while he is under investigation in Brazil for money laundering and tax evasion, which he denies.

The context the court order sits in: his brother Eduardo, who lives in self-imposed exile in Florida, had publicly suggested Flávio should leave Brazil if he lost. Their father, former president Jair Bolsonaro, is under house arrest serving 27 years for plotting a coup after losing to the same opponent in 2022.

The margin is worth restating because it is being contested. Lula won with just over 51 per cent — about 64.2 million valid votes — against more than 48 per cent, nearly 59.8 million: a gap of over four million. Bolsonaro's own Liberal Party has recognised the result. He has not conceded. President Trump has called the election "rigged" without offering evidence, and returned to it at a Texas rally on Tuesday night: "Brazil is also doing a good job of it, aren't they? Did you see that? I didn't take sides, but it didn't look very good."

Lula, marking his 81st birthday in Brasília, said of the reported departure: "Those who have nothing to hide have nothing to fear."

What to watch: whether Brazil confirms he left and how, which determines whether a court order was broken; whether the visa is actually issued and under what category; and whether this becomes an extradition question.

Israel has no majority, and the eighteen seats that could make one will not be asked

Geopolitics Continuation Independent corroboration

With about 72 per cent of votes counted by Israel's Central Elections Committee, the arithmetic is: Netanyahu's bloc 54 seats, Gadi Eisenkot's alliance 48, and the Arab parties 18. The Knesset has 120 seats and a government needs 61. Neither bloc has one.

Exit polls gave the same picture with different numbers — Channel 12 projected 52 for Netanyahu against 49 for Eisenkot's Yashar; Channel 13 had Likud and the opposition tied at 51 each. The counting has moved the margin without moving the conclusion, and the brief's own caution yesterday about Israeli exit polls diverging from the count has so far been unnecessary.

The 18 is the story. It is a historic result for the Arab parties and it is also the only route to 61 for either side: 54 plus 18 is 72, and 48 plus 18 is 66. Both numbers are governing majorities and neither is going to be formed, because no Israeli governing coalition has included an Arab party as a full partner except briefly in 2021, and this campaign made that less likely rather than more — Arab parties accused Netanyahu of inciting hatred to frighten voters to the polls. So an election with a clear arithmetic answer has no political one.

What produced it was participation. Turnout was the highest in roughly fifty years, in a vote widely read as a referendum on Netanyahu's leadership after the October 2023 Hamas attacks and the war in Gaza that followed, and amid escalating violence in the occupied West Bank. High turnout usually breaks a deadlock. Here it confirmed one.

What to watch: the final count and whether any party falls below the 3.25 per cent threshold, which can move several seats at once; who the President asks to form a government, and whether the mandate goes to the larger bloc or the likelier coalition; and whether anyone approaches the Arab parties at all, which would be the genuinely new development.

An executive order would cancel your mortgage insurance before you ask

US politics Macro & markets New subject Primary document

An executive order published this morning, "Decreasing Housing Costs for the American People", contains one provision with real money in it for existing homeowners. The FHFA Director is to direct Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to run a pilot that periodically reviews the single-family loans they own or guarantee, uses automated valuation models to assess current market value, identifies borrowers who now have enough equity to drop mortgage insurance — and cancels it proactively.

Private mortgage insurance protects the lender, is paid by the borrower, and is cancellable once equity reaches a threshold — but in practice the borrower has to know that, ask, and often pay for an appraisal. The order tells servicers to notify borrowers promptly and "reduce or eliminate unnecessary documentation requests, appraisal costs, and other administrative barriers". It applies only to mortgages made after the pilot is established, which limits it sharply, and it is conditioned throughout on the Director finding it in the Enterprises' and the public's interest.

The mechanism is an automated valuation model deciding what your house is worth. That is cheaper and faster than an appraisal and it is also a statistical estimate, which cuts both ways: it will cancel insurance for people who would never have asked, and it will decline to for people whose house the model misprices. The order requires safety-and-soundness standards and payment-history requirements, and leaves the model's accuracy to the Director.

Section 3 establishes a Task Force on Manufactured Housing Innovation, co-chaired by the HUD Secretary and the FHFA Director with Agriculture and Commerce, to standardise home permitting. And the policy statement in section 1 contains a phrase worth recording: the administration will "embrace innovative risk and value assessment technologies that incorporate Super Intelligence to drive down mortgage and homeownership costs" — which is discussed below the line.

What to watch: whether the FHFA Director makes the finding, since nothing happens until he does; the pilot's eligibility threshold and whether it matches the statutory 78 per cent; and whether the automated valuations are auditable by the borrower.

Confidence: Primary document, read in full for sections 1–3. The remaining sections, including any on competition and on the mortgage origination barriers named in section 1, were not read. The description of how PMI cancellation works today is general knowledge, not sourced from the order. Sources: FR 2026-22051 · GovInfo PDF

Wind turbines are now a national security threat, and nothing has been tariffed

Trade & supply chains Energy & grid New subject Primary document Underreported

A proclamation published this morning is titled "Adjusting Imports of Wind Turbines and Their Parts and Components Into the United States". Read the operative paragraphs and it adjusts no imports at all. It directs the Commerce Secretary and the Trade Representative to "pursue or continue pursuing negotiations", to update the President "from time to time" with at least one update within 180 days, and to keep monitoring. It expressly leaves existing section 232 tariffs unchanged. No duty is imposed, no rate is named, no annex is attached.

What it does do is make the finding, and the finding is the point. Under section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act, once the Secretary has reported and the President has accepted that imports "threaten to impair the national security", the authority to act exists. The investigation is the hard part and it is now complete. The 180-day clock in paragraph 2 is the tell: the statute lets the President act if an agreement is not reached within 180 days.

The reasoning placed on the record is remarkable for a trade instrument. The Secretary found that "wind energy is an unreliable and intermittent energy source and that the use of wind energy diverts the United States electrical grid from more reliable energy sources"; that wind's "unpredictability undermines wind's suitability as a standalone energy source compared to the steady output of fossil fuel or nuclear energy"; and that grid operators "must frequently rely upon fast-ramping fossil fuel backup generators" to manage it.

Alongside those are conventional security findings: foreign parts containing cybersecurity vulnerabilities "that could lead to remote control access to wind turbines", interference with national airspace and air safety, and radar interference affecting air traffic control, weather forecasting and defence missions. The cyber and radar findings are the kind section 232 is built for; the judgment that an energy source is too intermittent to be worth having is an energy policy, now written into a national security determination.

What to watch: whether a tariff follows at the 180-day mark, which falls around 20 April 2027; whether any agreement is actually negotiated and with whom; and whether the intermittency finding is cited in other proceedings, which is how a finding of this kind spreads.

Confidence: Primary document, read in full — the operative paragraphs are quoted and the absence of a tariff is a reading of the whole proclamation, not an inference. The Commerce Secretary's underlying section 232 report was not read and does not appear to be public, so the findings are as the proclamation recites them. Sources: FR 2026-22049 · GovInfo PDF

Tech & AI policy "Super Intelligence" is now a term in an executive order, and nobody has defined it.

Section 1 of today's housing executive order states the policy of embracing "innovative risk and value assessment technologies that incorporate Super Intelligence to drive down mortgage and homeownership costs". Capitalised, unglossed, in an operative policy statement about home valuation.

That is the third federal document in four days to use the term this way. On 25 October a Coast Guard notice announced a research agreement on "Super Intelligence (SI) Situational Awareness Tools" — which turned out to mean a camera company's object-detection software. On 27 October the Energy Department justified adding helium to the critical materials list partly because semiconductors are "essential for SI and data center supply chains". Now it appears in an executive order about mortgages.

Three agencies, three unrelated subjects, one undefined capitalised noun. In none of the three does the document say what Super Intelligence is, and in the one case where the meaning was recoverable it meant commercially available machine vision. There is also a "Super Intelligence legislative proposal" on this brief's calendar for around 28 November, which may or may not supply a definition. Until something does, a reader cannot tell whether these documents describe the same technology, three different ones, or a word that has become a synonym for "computers".

Below the line because a term of art is not an event. It is recorded because the phrase is being written into binding instruments faster than it is being defined, and the definition, when it arrives, will determine what those instruments reach.

Confidence: Primary documents, all three read. Sources: FR 2026-22051 · 27 October · 25 October

Regulatory The gift-reporting threshold for federal officials has gone up, retroactively, without comment.

The Office of Government Ethics has issued a final rule raising the dollar thresholds above which executive branch officials must report gifts and travel reimbursements, and raising the ceiling on the "widely attended gatherings" exception that lets officials accept a non-sponsor's hospitality. The threshold increase is retroactive to 1 January 2026; the gatherings change is not.

Two procedural facts. It was issued without notice and comment, on the stated grounds that these are technical amendments "unlikely to be of significant interest to the public", and it took immediate effect. And the thresholds rise by a statutory formula in the Ethics in Government Act — OGE is executing an indexation Congress wrote, not choosing a number, which is the fact that makes the procedure defensible.

It is still a rule that reduces how much federal officials must disclose about gifts, applied backwards across ten months, adopted on the view that the public would not be interested. The first two of those follow from the statute. The third is OGE's own characterisation, and it is the sort of sentence worth keeping.

Below the line because the substance is a formula and the discretion exercised was close to nil. It is here because the reporting threshold is the number that determines what anyone outside government ever learns about gifts to officials, and it moved today without anyone being asked.

Confidence: Primary document. The new dollar figures themselves sit in the regulatory text, which was not read; this brief reports the direction and the procedure, not the amounts. Sources: FR 2026-21997

Regulatory The DMCA's tenth triennial review proposes to renew every existing exemption but one.

The Copyright Office has opened the tenth triennial rulemaking under section 1201 of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act — the proceeding that decides which acts of breaking digital locks are lawful for the next three years. These are the exemptions that let people repair their own tractors and medical devices, preserve abandoned video games, caption films, do security research, and extract text for academic study.

The Register intends to recommend all but one of the existing exemptions for renewal. The document does not, in its summary, say which one. Renewal is normally the uncontroversial half of the proceeding, so a single exclusion is where the dispute is, and identifying it requires reading past the summary — which this brief did not do.

Three rounds of comment follow: proponents and neutral parties by 7 December 2026, opponents by 22 February 2027, replies by 22 March 2027, with a twenty-five-page limit per class excluding evidence. That cadence — four months of structured adversarial exchange — is unusual in federal rulemaking and is why the section 1201 process produces a better record than most comment files.

Below the line because it is the opening of a proceeding that will not conclude until late 2027, and because the one fact that would make it a story — which exemption is being dropped — is the one not yet established here.

Confidence: Primary document, summary only of roughly 160,000 characters. Sources: FR 2026-21939
