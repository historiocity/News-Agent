OpenAI and Synopsys will build GPT-Synopsys to run the tools that design chips

AI & tech industry New subject Underreported Primary source

Synopsys’s investor release of 30 September — missed by Issues 27–28-grok and not on Friday’s Cook/stress-test menu — announces a multi-year preferred-partner agreement with OpenAI to develop GPT-Synopsys: a specialised model trained to operate Synopsys electronic-design-automation tools for semiconductor design and verification. OpenAI will license Synopsys EDA tools to train the model; the companies will share revenue and jointly go to market. The model is to run on OpenAI-hosted infrastructure, integrate with Synopsys.ai and Synopsys Autopilot, and accept engineer-delegated objectives (PPA optimisation, timing and verification closure) with agents iterating tool runs for human review. Customer design data is not used for training; encrypted at rest and in transit.

This is the AI buildout turning inward on its own constraint: chip design throughput. Greg Brockman’s framing in the announcement is recursive — better chips for better models — and Sassine Ghazi’s is commercial — more companies reaching advanced silicon without cannibalising Synopsys’s tool business. Early customer technology engagements are “underway”; no ship date, no disclosed dollars.

What to watch: whether a named foundry or fabless customer confirms a production pilot; any BIS/export-control attention on frontier models that directly drive EDA; and how revenue-share economics show up in Synopsys’s next earnings.

By executive order, the federal government will say “Super Intelligence,” not “AI”

AI & tech industry US politics & policy Primary source

Executive Order 14434 of 29 September — published in the Federal Register Friday as Doc. 2026-20321 — orders that, to the maximum extent permitted by law, the executive branch shall use “Super Intelligence” / “SI” in place of “Artificial Intelligence” / “AI” in official correspondence, websites, reports and other non-statutory documents, and “will not acknowledge the usage” of the older terms in applicable settings. Previously issued regulations, contracts and historical documents need not be rewritten. For now, SI is defined as whatever 15 U.S.C. §9401(3) already means by artificial intelligence.

The operative second half is the homework: within 60 days the Assistant to the President for Science and Technology must propose legislative language for a statutory SI definition, including whether it should modify or supersede the existing AI statute, plus conforming amendments. That is nomenclature becoming a bill-drafting assignment — not merely a style guide.

What to watch: the 60-day legislative package; whether agencies actually stop saying “AI” in live web copy; and how the rename interacts with the voluntary lab pact (next story) that still talks in the old vocabulary.

Dig deeper — what the order does and does not change

Section 1’s purpose language frames frontier systems as already beyond “imitate or automate discrete aspects of human intelligence.” Section 2 is the rename. Section 3 keeps the statutory AI definition until Congress or a later Presidential action replaces it, then tasks APST with proposed legislation. Section 4 is the standard severability / no-private-right boiler plate; OSTP bears publication costs. Read whole: this is branding plus a statutory-definition work order, not a safety standard, compute limit, or liability rule.

FY2027 refugee ceiling is 17,500, allocated primarily to Afrikaners from South Africa

US politics & policy New subject Primary source

Presidential Determination 2026-24 of 25 September — FR Doc. 2026-20318, published Friday — sets Fiscal Year 2027 refugee admissions at up to 17,500. The text directs that admissions “shall primarily be allocated among Afrikaners from South Africa pursuant to Executive Order 14204.” Admissions remain subject to EO 14161 (stringent identity verification), EO 14163 (suspension of refugee entry except national-interest determinations by State and DHS), EO 14204 itself, and Proclamation 10998. Separately, persons processed under EO 14204 may, if otherwise qualified, be considered refugees while still in their countries of nationality or habitual residence.

The number and the allocation rule are the story; advocacy characterisations of who “Afrikaners” are for EO 14204 purposes are downstream of that text and are not re-litigated here. The ceiling is a statutory INA §207 instrument, not a press conference.

What to watch: State/DHS joint national-interest exceptions under EO 14163; actual FY2027 arrivals versus the 17,500 ceiling; and any litigation over the primary-allocation language.

EO 14433 orders a 90% cut in invasive mosquitoes — in Washington, DC — while Hillsborough counts 223 dengue cases

Science & public health US politics & policy Primary source

Executive Order 14433 (FR Doc. 2026-20320) directs Interior, Agriculture, EPA and GSA — consulting HHS — to develop and implement a campaign across the District of Columbia to cut Asian tiger and Egyptian (Aedes) mosquito populations 90% and ticks 50% by 2028, with sterile-insect and other non-chemical tools prioritised, then to expand successful techniques nationwide. EPA gets 60 days for a regulatory framework on advanced control technologies (aligned with the biological-research EO). HHS gets 90 days for diagnostics/prophylactics/therapeutics prize competitions covering dengue among other mosquito- and tick-borne diseases. The order’s funded geography for the population cut is DC — explicitly tied to the DC Safe and Beautiful task force.

That geography is the editorial point beside Florida’s live outbreak (next story). The federal 90% target is not aimed at Hillsborough’s 223 locally acquired dengue cases; the nationwide “model” language is aspirational follow-on.
