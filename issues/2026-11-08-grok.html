CENTCOM opened a 20-person Tampa cell to squeeze Iran’s land and Caspian routes

Geopolitics — US channel Florida & Tampa Bay New subject Primary source

On Saturday, Admiral Brad Cooper directed U.S. Central Command to form Task Force Iron Gate, an internal staff of about 20 civilian and military people based in Tampa. The command’s own release says the cell will work with other U.S. agencies to stop advanced weapons, dual-use technologies, sanctioned materials and what it calls illegal revenue from moving across Iran’s land borders and the Caspian Sea. Cooper said the task force “reinforces the success of our ironclad blockade against Iran at sea.”

That is the point of the announcement. The naval blockade, in CENTCOM’s account, has already stopped Iran from exporting more than $25 billion of crude. A Reuters investigation, reported by CNBC, found Iran had built a separate inland network that has brought in at least $2 billion of hardware, explosives and heavy trucks since January 2025, moving cargo by ship across the Caspian from Russia, by truck from Central Asia and by freight aircraft from China. A U.S. official, speaking without attribution, told CNBC the new cell is expected to pull in Treasury, State, Justice and the intelligence agencies. This is a staff, not a new combat formation. Twenty people in Tampa cannot themselves seal a border. What they can do is give the agencies that write designations and pick targets a single place to work.

Michael Mulroy, a former deputy assistant defence secretary for the Middle East, told Al Jazeera the cell’s job is to find where weapons are coming in and how Iran moves energy. If the President orders an escalation, he said, the targets are more likely to be economic and infrastructure sites than the military ones that Operation Epic Fury has hit. That is his reading, not CENTCOM’s. The release itself does not announce new strikes.

For this reader the geography is local as well as strategic. CENTCOM is at MacDill Air Force Base. The war’s next coordination shop is being stood up in Tampa.

What to watch: a Treasury or State designation that cites a land or Caspian route, or a strike on such a route that the command ties to this cell.

Divergence — a coordination shop or a target list?

What is disputed. A dispute of significance. CENTCOM describes a 20-person internal staff that will coordinate and support existing efforts. Mulroy describes a cell that will identify where to cut weapons and energy flows, and that could supply targets if the war expands.

Why they differ. The mundane explanation is vantage. A command release describes the unit it has just created, in the most limited terms that still justify the announcement. Mulroy is reading the same text as a former Pentagon official and asking what it is for. Timing matters too: the announcement landed the same day U.S. aircraft disabled a cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman, so it arrived inside an enforcement story rather than as a quiet organogram change.

What it means for you. Take the release as the fact: a Tampa staff of about 20, with an interagency brief covering land borders and the Caspian. Treat Mulroy’s escalation reading as informed speculation until a designation or a strike shows what the cell actually produces. The $25 billion export figure is CENTCOM’s own and has not been audited here against an independent tanker series.

Three tankers, a disabled cargo ship, shrapnel on an Aramco tower, and no Russian diesel at the dock

Energy — one causal chain Follow-up Independent corroboration

A correction of a date. Yesterday’s oil chain said EIA’s weekly retail prices were due Monday. The table’s own next-release date is Tuesday, 10 November. The Tuesday cycle was recorded in this brief’s standing notes on 28 September and was not applied.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said three oil tankers caught fire early Sunday on what they called an unauthorized route in the Strait of Hormuz. The Guards said the ships had switched off their navigation and identification systems and were accompanying U.S. forces, and warned that vessels which defy the rules “will have no fate other than this.” They did not name the ships or their flags. A day earlier the same navy said two tankers had hit mines in the strait. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations office said Sunday that a tanker caught fire Saturday off Khasab, Oman, and that the crew was evacuated, and that another tanker was struck by an unknown projectile the same day. Emirati state media said 22 people were rescued from a burning vessel in the Gulf of Oman and were stable. Agence France-Presse could not say whether those reports described the same incidents.

Separately, and this one has a name, CENTCOM said Saturday that a U.S. military aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of the St. Kitts-flagged cargo ship M/V Huda in the Gulf of Oman after the crew ignored warnings and tried to leave regional waters following a call at an Iranian port. The command says that since July it has redirected 147 commercial vessels that complied and disabled five that did not, and that it had earlier destroyed ten Iranian commercial oil tankers in the Revolutionary Guards’ shadow network. The Huda is a cargo ship, not a tanker. Do not add it to the IRGC’s count.

In Riyadh on Sunday, Saudi Arabia’s Energy Ministry said an Aramco administrative building in the King Abdullah Financial District took minor damage from falling shrapnel. No injuries were reported. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted two ballistic missiles and two cruise missiles. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said on Saturday that the group had carried out six operations against King Khalid International Airport, Aramco sites in Riyadh and Khurais, and Pumping Station No. 8 in Dawadmi. An administrative tower is not a production facility, but it is the company, in the capital, on a Sunday.

The policy that was supposed to ease the pump is not landing barrels. Nearly a month after President Trump said Russia would release diesel to U.S. and world markets, Al Jazeera and the AP report that no Russian diesel has reached a U.S. port. Kpler and MarineTraffic show the Talisman and the Sierra Leone-flagged Leruo turning toward Brazil and the Mediterranean; a third ship changed its destination from New York to “for orders.” Congress’s ban on Russian energy imports is still on the books. New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill told port operators in her state to turn the ships away. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said “all the diesel will find its buyers.” Kirill Dmitriev called the diversion reports fake.

AAA’s national average on Sunday, 8 November, is $4.0203 for regular, down from $4.0371 on Saturday and $4.1732 a week ago, and $6.1929 for diesel, down from $6.2075 and $6.3039. A year ago those figures were $3.0731 and $3.7286. EIA’s weekly series, latest week ending 2 November, has all-grades gasoline at $4.290 and on-highway diesel at $6.292, both down on the week. ICE Brent futures settled Friday at $98.32 a barrel, up 3 cents (The Star, from the settlement feed). That is the futures contract, not Dated Brent.

What to watch: named tankers and a UKMTO or insurer confirmation; whether any of the Russian cargoes actually discharge at a U.S. terminal; EIA’s next weekly print on Tuesday, 10 November.

Divergence — which ships, and whether the diesel is coming

What is disputed. Two factual points. First, the tanker count and the cause: the Guards said two ships hit mines on Saturday and three were ablaze on Sunday; UKMTO and UAE media describe fires and a projectile strike without adopting the Guards’ numbers or the mine claim; AFP could not match the reports to the same hulls. Second, the Russian diesel: tracking firms show course changes away from the U.S. East Coast, while Dmitriev says the reports are fake and the tankers remain bound for America.

Why they differ. The Guards are issuing claims without vessel names, which makes matching impossible from here. UKMTO is a shipping-safety desk; it reports what masters and naval authorities tell it, often a day later. The 22 people rescued in the Gulf of Oman may be the Huda’s crew after the U.S. strike, which would be a cargo ship and not one of the Guards’ tankers. On the diesel, Kpler and MarineTraffic are reading AIS positions; Dmitriev is selling a deal he helped arrange. Timing also splits the accounts: Saturday’s two and Sunday’s three may be a running tally of the same fires.

What it means for you. Treat the three-tanker claim as a claim until a ship is named or UKMTO confirms it. Treat the Huda as a documented U.S. disabling of a cargo vessel, counted by CENTCOM among the five that refused to comply. Treat the Aramco damage as the ministry described it: shrapnel on an administrative building, not a hit on output. On diesel, the AIS turns are the measurement; a discharge at a U.S. dock would settle Dmitriev’s denial. The pump is still easing on the daily card and on the weekly survey, from levels far above a year ago.

Dig deeper — why the failed diesel deal belongs in the same chain as Hormuz

This brief treats oil, strikes and the pump as one story when they are one shock. The shock is still the war: a blocked strait, a U.S. blockade, Houthi fire at Saudi energy symbols, and a White House search for barrels that do not have to transit Hormuz. The October arrangement with Moscow was the policy answer to the price. Trump said after a 9 October call that Russia would make more than 300,000 tonnes of diesel available at once, then 500,000 tonnes in November and a million after that, and that sales could run until 7 April. Treasury has issued a temporary sanctions exemption and says it complies with applicable law. Congress’s import ban was never repealed. That legal overlap is why traders will not bring the cargoes ashore, and why a governor can tell her ports to refuse them without waiting for a court. Aarathi Krishnan of Raksha Intelligence told Al Jazeera the first reported shipment was about two hours of U.S. distillate use. Even if one ship arrived, it would not reset the national average.

Friday’s ICE Brent settlement of $98.32 sits near the level this brief printed yesterday from a different feed on a different day (Trading Economics had $97.76 on 6 November). Those are not a contradiction. Named instruments, named dates. Dated Brent, the physical cargo assessment, has traded far above futures when ships are hard to charter; this run did not obtain a Dated Brent print.

Herzog handed Eisenkot 28 days to form a government, and Netanyahu did not get the first try

Geopolitics — US channel New subject Independent corroboration

President Isaac Herzog announced Saturday evening that he would task Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party, with forming Israel’s next government. Eisenkot was to receive the mandate formally on Sunday afternoon at the President’s Residence. He then has 28 days, and Herzog may add up to 14 more. If he fails, Benjamin Netanyahu could be given the next chance.

During Herzog’s consultations, Eisenkot received 67 recommendations for the premiership in the 120-seat Knesset. Netanyahu received 46. United Torah Judaism, with seven seats, made no recommendation. Recommendations are not coalition votes. Eisenkot’s change bloc has 48 seats. Getting to 61 still depends on ultra-Orthodox parties, Arab-led parties, or a slice of Likud without Netanyahu himself. Eisenkot has said he will not sit with Netanyahu, and that anyone who held a major position of power on 7 October 2023 should leave public life. He wrote that “now is the time for the formation of a national emergency government.”

Netanyahu, in a video on Saturday, called for a national unity government that would include all of his current allies — Religious Zionism, Otzma Yehudit and the ultra-Orthodox parties — which is the coalition Eisenkot’s partners campaigned against. He warned against a government backed by Arab parties and said a unity government would “permanently remove the nuclear threat from Iran.” Yashar said Likud could join an Eisenkot government without the extremists, and without naming Netanyahu. Avigdor Liberman said on Friday he would accept painful compromises with the ultra-Orthodox to form a government that excludes both Netanyahu and the Arab parties. Channel 12 reported that if United Torah Judaism’s rabbis approved such a deal, Shas would not oppose it; Shas called reports that it might join “total fake news.”

The U.S. channel is the war this brief already covers. Israel is Washington’s partner in the campaign against Iran. Who holds the mandate in Jerusalem is who Washington will be dealing with if the Qatari track, or another round of strikes, requires an Israeli decision. Defence Minister Israel Katz said Sunday that with regard to Iran, “the mission is still not complete.”

What to watch: whether United Torah Judaism publicly enters talks, and whether Eisenkot’s 28 days produce a majority that does not depend on a last-minute surprise.

Divergence — a majority of recommendations is not a government

What is disputed. A dispute of significance, with a factual edge on Shas. Herzog’s 67-to-46 split is being read in some accounts as Eisenkot already having a majority. He has a majority of party recommendations, not a signed coalition. Separately, unnamed sources told Israeli channels that Shas would at least abstain if United Torah Judaism joined Eisenkot; the party itself called reports that it would join fake news.

Why they differ. Recommendations are a presidential consultation ritual, not a confidence vote. Outlets that lead on “67” are describing Herzog’s inputs. Outlets that stress the fraught path are counting the 48 seats Eisenkot actually brings and the partners he still has to persuade. On Shas, declining to oppose and agreeing to join are different statements, and a denial of the second does not kill the first.

What it means for you. Eisenkot has the first try. He does not yet have a government. A published coalition agreement, or a failure that sends the mandate back to Netanyahu, would settle it. Until then, treat Israeli policy toward Iran as continuous with the outgoing cabinet, not as already rewritten.

Berkshire bought back $9.6 billion of its own stock in a quarter when operating earnings fell

US financials New subject Primary source

Berkshire Hathaway’s own news release, dated 7 November and posted on the company’s site, says the firm acquired about $9.6 billion of treasury shares in the third quarter. That brought the nine-month total to about $14.4 billion. Operating earnings, which Berkshire defines as net earnings without investment gains and losses, were $12.705 billion, against $13.485 billion a year earlier. Net earnings attributable to shareholders were $22.112 billion, against $30.796 billion, reflecting smaller investment gains ($9.407 billion against $17.311 billion).

The operating decline sat in insurance. Underwriting earned $1.352 billion, against $2.369 billion. Insurance investment income was $2.734 billion, against $3.181 billion. The rest of the shop grew: BNSF made $1.729 billion against $1.449 billion; the energy business $1.612 billion against $1.489 billion; manufacturing, service and retailing $4.042 billion against $3.616 billion. At 30 September there were 1,418,900 Class A equivalent shares outstanding. Insurance float was about $178.5 billion, up about $2.1 billion since the end of 2025. The release tells readers the figures in it are not enough for an investment decision and points them to the 10-Q. This account is written from the release. The 10-Q itself was not walked through line by line.

Secondary accounts add two numbers the release does not. Reuters, carried by Euronext, says Berkshire bought about $2 billion more of its shares in October, which some write-ups add to the $14.4 billion to get a year-to-date total near $16.4 billion. CNBC puts cash at $377.435 billion at the end of September, or $352.3 billion after certain adjustments; Reuters puts cash and equivalents at $356.9 billion. Those cash figures are not in the release I read.

The usefulness of the print is what it says about Greg Abel’s capital allocation in his third quarter as chief executive. Buying $9.6 billion of the company’s own stock, more than in the first half combined, is a judgement that the shares were cheap relative to the cash on the balance sheet. Handelsblatt noted that the stock has been helped by the same AI-share unease that hit the Nasdaq last week, because Berkshire is still widely read as a place to stand when the growth trade is being marked down.

What to watch: the 10-Q cash line, and whether the October buybacks Reuters reported show up in the next 10-Q.

Divergence — how much cash, and how much has been spent this year

What is disputed. Two factual points, both about figures that are not in the press release. First, year-to-date buybacks: the company says about $14.4 billion through nine months. Some accounts add about $2 billion in October and print roughly $16.4 billion. Second, the cash pile: $377 billion and about $357 billion are both in circulation.

Why they differ. The $14.4 billion and the $9.6 billion are the company’s own nine-month and third-quarter figures. October sits outside that table, so a writer who includes it is using a later, secondary tally. On cash, a gross figure near $377 billion and a lower figure after subtracting unsettled Treasury-bill purchases is a standard Berkshire presentation and would explain a gap of about $20 billion without anyone being wrong.

What it means for you. Use the release: $12.705 billion of operating earnings, $9.6 billion of third-quarter buybacks, $14.4 billion through September. Do not take a cash level as established until the 10-Q line is read. The October buybacks are plausible and still second-hand.

Incoming Democrats say they will subpoena the architects of the deportation campaign over fatal shootings

US politics & policy New subject Single-sourced

NPR reported on Saturday that Democrats, having taken both chambers, plan to use subpoena power to investigate fatal shootings by federal immigration agents. The named cases are the January killings in Minneapolis of Renee Good, a mother of three and a U.S. citizen, by an ICE officer, and of Alex Pretti, a VA nurse and a U.S. citizen, by Border Patrol agents; and the July killing in Houston of Lorenzo Salgado Araujo, a father of three and a Mexican immigrant. NPR says federal officials have given little public sign of progress in those investigations, or in the deaths of at least seven other people killed by federal immigration agents since the President returned to office. The Department of Homeland Security did not respond to NPR’s question about the status of the inquiries.

With a majority, Democrats can compel testimony under oath from the people who designed and ran the campaign. NPR lists White House border czar Tom Homan, former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, former Border Patrol commander Gregory Bovino, and incoming chief of staff Stephen Miller, and says they can also demand use-of-force reports. Representative Jamie Raskin, expected to lead House Judiciary, told NPR it is time for ICE to “start answering to the American people.” Representative Robert Garcia, expected to lead Oversight, issued a two-word statement: “Lawyer Up.” Representative Bennie Thompson, expected to lead Homeland Security, wrote to Secretary Markwayne Mullin on Wednesday demanding that the department preserve documents.

DHS general counsel James Percival said the department is prepared to deal with what he called nonsense. A White House spokesperson told NPR that Democrats “should be reminded that we still control the federal government and all of its powers.” NPR notes that Congress has powers of its own, including the power to compel testimony. Rachel Moran, a law professor at the University of St. Thomas, told NPR that clawing back ICE funding would still need a veto-proof majority, which Democrats do not have, but that a deeper probe can put evidence in the hands of state investigators. Minnesota officials have been fighting the federal government for evidence in the Good and Pretti cases.

This is the first concrete oversight plan from the incoming majority that this brief has been able to read in full. It lands as Miller prepares to take over as chief of staff on Monday, a change this brief covered yesterday. He is on the list of people Democrats say they will call.

What to watch: the first subpoena, and whether DHS produces the Minneapolis files Minnesota has asked for.

Trump met Putin’s envoy and forecast a deal; the dated fact is talks in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November

Geopolitics — US channel New subject Independent corroboration

President Trump said he met privately on Thursday with Kirill Dmitriev, Vladimir Putin’s envoy, after Dmitriev appeared at a White House event that Melania Trump hosted with Ukrainian representatives. He would not say what they discussed. He did say the war was close to being settled and that ending it might involve more than Ukraine. “It’s going to be a big, beautiful agreement,” he told reporters, and that everyone would be thrilled. He said he did not meet the Ukrainian delegation that day.

The dated item underneath the prediction is a round of U.S.-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November between Russian and Ukrainian representatives. Jared Kushner said U.S. officials in Brussels on Thursday held discussions with Ukraine, NATO and European partners on security guarantees. He listed five aims: permanent security consultations, reliable crisis communications, greater military transparency, reciprocal steps to reduce the risk of escalation, and a longer-term European security framework. He said any arrangement must respect each country’s sovereignty and that no country would have a veto over another’s alliances. NATO, he said, would remain central to European defence.

Thursday’s White House event marked a six-day humanitarian ceasefire in Russian-controlled Oleshky that the first lady helped arrange. That ceasefire expired on Sunday. No text of a broader settlement has been published. This brief has a standing rule, written after four issues inferred too much from a missing Trump–Xi readout: when the document that would settle a question has not appeared, report the absence and stop. There is a meeting on the calendar. There is not a published agreement.

A Democratic Congress, seated in January, is widely expected to push for more aid to Ukraine and to question the diesel arrangement with Moscow described in the oil chain above. The Kremlin has said it hopes the midterm result will not change Trump’s approach, and that preparations are underway for a meeting of Putin, Trump and Xi Jinping at APEC in Shenzhen later this month.

What to watch: whether Abu Dhabi produces a text, even a partial one, and whether Shenzhen includes that three-leader meeting.

Divergence — a deal that is close, or a process that has a date?

What is disputed. A dispute of significance. The President says a settlement is near and may cover all of Europe. Kushner describes working-level talks about consultations, communications and military transparency. No source has produced terms on territory, security guarantees with force behind them, or sanctions.

Why they differ. They are describing different objects. Trump is talking to reporters after a private meeting and has predicted an imminent deal many times before, as Reuters noted. Kushner is describing the agenda of officials in Brussels. Timing also splits them: a humanitarian ceasefire that has already expired is not a political settlement, though it is being presented in the same week.

What it means for you. Believe the calendar and the process list. Do not believe that a settlement exists. A published text, or a named territorial or security term accepted by both Kyiv and Moscow, would change that. Silence after Abu Dhabi would mean the prediction has slipped again.

Trump ordered Justice to investigate Democrats for “threatening letters” he did not identify

US politics & policy New subject Independent corroboration

On Saturday the President wrote on Truth Social that he was ordering the Justice Department to investigate Democratic leaders over what he described as threats against Republican officials, donors and supporters. He said Democrats were threatening a lot of people, including the attorney general, with impeachment and other punishment, “even though they admit they have no reason to do so.” He called the conduct “extortion at the highest level” and wrote, “I am ordering the Department of Justice to look into this criminal act, immediately!” He also wrote that threatening letters had been sent to many innocent people by Democratic political leaders. He did not name the letters, the senders or the recipients, and the accounts of the post that this brief read provide no independent evidence of those communications.

In the same post he said Senator Elissa Slotkin of Michigan planned to go after young people who believe in Republican values. CNBC reports that he appeared to be referring to a 13 October speech at the University of Chicago in which Slotkin said she wanted young graduates to think twice before taking jobs in the administration, and to see that choice as buying problems they did not want. That is a political deterrence message, not a criminal threat, on the words CNBC quotes.

The order arrives on top of an earlier Justice Department inquiry into a November 2025 video in which Slotkin and other Democrats urged service members to refuse illegal orders after U.S. strikes on boats in the Caribbean. Trump called that video treason and said an example must be set. The FBI contacted lawmakers. In February, Slotkin declined a Justice Department interview. “The intimidation is the point, and I’m not gonna go along with that,” she said then. A federal grand jury later declined to indict the participants. CNBC said the White House and Slotkin did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Saturday’s order.

Impeachment is a constitutional power of Congress, not a crime. An incoming Democratic majority talking about oversight, or even about impeachment, is doing something the Constitution contemplates. A president instructing his own Justice Department to treat that talk as extortion is a different act, and it is the act that happened on Saturday. Whether the department opens a matter is the next fact.

What to watch: a case number, a named target, or a public statement from the department that no inquiry has been opened.

Divergence — an order to investigate, or evidence of a crime?

What is disputed. A factual point underneath a real order. The President says threatening letters exist and that the conduct is criminal extortion. No reachable account identifies a letter, a sender or a recipient. The Slotkin speech CNBC cites is about discouraging people from taking administration jobs.

Why they differ. The President is describing, in his own words, a pattern he wants investigated. Newsrooms can confirm that he gave the order because he published it. They cannot confirm the letters without the documents. The earlier video inquiry supplies the context: this department has already examined Democratic speech at the President’s urging and failed to win an indictment.

What it means for you. The order is real. The letters are unconfirmed. Believe that he told Justice to look; do not believe that a crime has been shown. A filed matter with a named subject, or a declination, would settle the second question.

Sources: CNBC · Al Jazeera · Politico
