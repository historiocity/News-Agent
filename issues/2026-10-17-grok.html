Correction vs Claude Issue 43

Claude’s Saturday dormant note said the Graham Act implementation deadline is Sunday 18 Oct and that nothing had been filed. The White House published the implementing EO on Friday 16 Oct — primary text read here. Handelsblatt’s weekend front page led the same fact in German (“setzt neue Russland-Sanktionen per Dekret aus”).

The order (primary). On 16 Oct President Trump signed Implementing the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026. Section 2 states the statutory duty increases — 500% ad valorem on goods from the Russian Federation and 100% on goods from the largest five importers of Russian crude/gas (and on the top five facilitators of Russian oil-sanctions evasion) — then Section 4 waives them. Under §115 of the Act he certifies to Congress that the waiver is in the national interests of the United States. The waiver also covers the Act’s new sanctions and restrictions in §§102(a), 102(d), 102(e), 103–111, and 117. Existing Russia sanctions under earlier executive orders are expressly preserved. The waivers remain until the President or named cabinet officials “determine that further action is appropriate.” The Act was signed 18 Sep; the duty clock ran toward Sunday 18 Oct.

On Monday the CFTC takes casino-style gambling out of the “swap” definition and proposes to put sports-, politics-, and weather-event contracts in

US financials US policy Underreported New fact

Saturday FR pre-read (label: unpublished until Monday). Two CFTC documents are already in Monday 19 Oct’s Federal Register package (API count confirmed; full-text endpoints readable today):

Interim final rule 2026-21376 — Further Definition of “Swap” To Exclude Casino-Style Gambling Products (RIN 3038-AF81). Effective 19 Oct 2026. Codifies the Commission’s longstanding position that casino-style gambling products, including sportsbook wagers and casino games, are excluded from the “swap” definition. Comments due 18 Nov.

Proposed rule 2026-21377 — Further Definition of “Swap”—Inclusions (RIN 3038-AF82). Proposes to further define “swap” to explicitly include event contracts, including those based on sports-, politics-, cultural-, and weather-related events. Same comment deadline.

Meeks and a bipartisan slate introduce the Prohibit Corrupt Russia Oil Deals Act to nullify OFAC’s Russian diesel licences

US politics & policy Energy New fact

The bill (16 Oct, primary). House Foreign Affairs Ranking Member Gregory Meeks introduced the bipartisan Prohibit Corrupt Russia Oil Deals Act to block the administration’s Russian diesel arrangement. Per the committee press release (read): the bill would (1) prohibit the US International Development Finance Corporation from investing in Russian energy; (2) nullify OFAC general licences authorizing Russian fuel transactions and bar future ones; and (3) mandate sanctions on Russian energy-sector entities. Cosponsors listed include Democrats plus Republican Don Bacon (NE-02).

Why it is here. It is the Hill’s same-day answer to the diesel licence and to Friday’s Graham Act waiver (lead 1) — substance, not horse-race. It is introduced, not enacted. Pair with the bipartisan senators’ earlier letter urging enforcement of Graham Act tools (search/X news summaries).
