Jensen Huang has resigned as Nvidia’s chief executive, and the intelligence agencies still disagree on whether the woman at the centre of it was a spy

AI & tech industry Follow-up Independent corroboration

Nvidia announced on Thursday morning that Huang had resigned as chief executive and from the board, ending a 33-year tenure. Chief financial officer Colette Kress, a 13-year veteran, is interim CEO; the board says it is weighing internal and external candidates and has named no succession plan. The trigger was a second relationship reported on Wednesday, with Kimberly Powell, a vice president who reported to him, on top of last weekend’s disclosure of an affair with Alice Huang (no relation), a Singapore-based businesswoman US agencies believe was a Chinese intelligence asset.

The market price: shares closed down 4.3 per cent at $166.19 after falling as much as 14.3 per cent intraday, and CBS puts the loss since last week’s close at almost $500 billion. The policy price is larger. Huang was the industry’s most effective lobbyist against tighter export controls, and Republicans are now turning on Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over Megaspeed, the chip firm linked to Alice Huang that, by one House Republican’s account, Trump directed three times be blacklisted. Huang has agreed to testify before Congress, and GOP members want Lutnick in the same chair.

Accountability. Yesterday’s edition led on the board meeting to decide whether to remove him. That question is now answered; the open one is the security case, below.

Divergence — was Alice Huang actually a spy?

What is disputed. A facts dispute. The premise that drove a week of coverage and the share-price fall is that US intelligence assessed her as a Chinese asset. CNN, citing multiple officials, reports that some at the Office of the Director of National Intelligence call that characterisation over-torqued and that CIA officials are not convinced the intercepts show she knowingly passed information to Beijing. Other administration officials say it strains belief that she was not.

Why they differ. Mundane first: agencies are reviewing the same intercepts separately, and an assessment of risk is not a finding of espionage. Attorney General Blanche said on Thursday that no conclusion has been reached, and Trump said he did not know whether it had been determined. Interest matters too: tech allies of Huang (David Sacks called the narrative collapsing) have a stake in the weaker reading; China hawks pressing Lutnick have a stake in the stronger one.

What it means for you. Treat “spy” as unproven and “counterintelligence risk” as well supported; the resignation stands on the undisclosed relationship with a direct report regardless. What would settle it: a DOJ charging decision, or a declassified ODNI assessment for the hearings.

The 10-year Treasury yield is back above 5.10 per cent, and markets now treat a December rate rise as certain

Macro & markets Follow-up Primary source

Treasury’s own par curve for 12 November has the 10-year at 5.10 per cent and the 2-year at 4.86, up 12 and 13 basis points from the last session before Veterans Day; the 30-year is 5.43. Dow Jones data put the 10-year’s four-day rise at 27 basis points, the largest since late September, and the level the highest since 21 October. Short maturities rose most, which is the signature of a market repricing the Fed rather than the deficit.

The Fed path: CME FedWatch and Kalshi both price a December rise as near-certain, with roughly one chance in eight that it is half a point. Governor Waller said on 10 November that a core CPI reading near 0.3 per cent would put 50 basis points on the table; October core came in at exactly 0.3. Goldman added a March rise to its forecast. Separately, Governor Barr warned that deregulating in a “risk-on environment” is a recipe for trouble.

Why it matters to you: this is the rate mortgages, car loans and corporate refinancing key off, and it moved the same day the housing figures below landed. The 30-year auction drew weak demand, per Yonhap’s market report.

Divergence — was this an oil selloff or a Fed selloff?

What is disputed. A significance dispute over cause. CNBC and Yonhap Infomax lead with oil: Trump’s “we don’t need a deal” post lifted Brent about 3 per cent and yields climbed through the session. Bloomberg leads with the Fed: traders bracing for a faster pace of rises after a hot CPI and hawkish officials.

Why they differ. Timing and vantage. Wednesday was a bond-market holiday, so Thursday absorbed two days of news at once — Tuesday’s CPI rout, the Nvidia shock (which briefly pulled the 10-year down to 4.88 at 8 a.m.) and Trump’s post. Intraday desks see the post move prices; macro desks see the curve flatten.

What it means for you. The curve settles it better than the narratives: the 2-year rose more than the 10-year, so most of the move was Fed expectations, with oil adding to it. If Brent falls back and yields do not, that confirms it.

Trump says the US no longer needs a deal with Iran; Brent jumped to $96 and Iran claims a strike on a US radar in Bahrain

Energy & oil Follow-up Independent corroboration

One chain, three links. On Thursday Trump posted that “we don’t need a deal with Iran,” three days after calling the war militarily over. Brent, which had fallen about 25 per cent from its late-October high of $118 on peace expectations, rose roughly 3.7 per cent to about $96; WTI closed at $88.74. Overnight, after state media reported “enemy fire” on a telecommunications tower in Sirik on the Hormuz coast, the Revolutionary Guards said they had targeted and destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, home of the Fifth Fleet — the first such claim against US assets in a Gulf state since early September. AP says the Guards also reported a tanker hitting a mine in the strait.

The paperwork moved too. Today’s Federal Register carries the President’s periodic determination, dated 28 October, that non-Iranian oil supply is sufficient to keep sanctions pressure on Iranian crude purchases — the legal predicate for the sanctions regime staying in place regardless of talks. The same issue publishes OFAC’s new Iran-related general licences.

At the pump, AAA’s national average fell below $4 for the first time since July, to $3.90, down nearly 20 cents in a week — still the most expensive November on record.

Divergence — did Iran hit anything in Bahrain?

What is disputed. A facts dispute. The Guards and Fars say the radar was destroyed. AP, Reuters, RFE/RL and Al Jazeera all say the claim could not be verified, and neither Washington nor Manama had commented by this morning.

Why they differ. Access and interest: Iranian state outlets are the only source, and the claim serves as a deterrent signal whatever happened. RFE/RL notes the Guards’ statement stops short of blaming the US for the Sirik strike, which suggests calibration rather than escalation.

What it means for you. Treat the strike as Unconfirmed. The market relevance is the rhetoric on both sides, not the radar. A CENTCOM statement or commercial satellite imagery would settle it.

Divergence — crude is up, so why is gasoline down?

What is disputed. A significance dispute between data series. Oil reports show crude rising on Thursday; AAA shows the pump at a four-month low.

Why they differ. Timing: retail prices lag crude by one to two weeks, and AAA cites the switch to cheaper winter-blend fuel and lower autumn demand. AAA’s figures reflect the 25 per cent crude slide since late October, not Thursday’s bounce.

What it means for you. Expect the decline at the pump to slow or stall next week if Brent holds near $96. If Brent goes back toward $90, the fall continues.

Existing home sales fell to their lowest rate since August 2010

Macro & markets New subject Primary source

The National Association of Realtors reported October resales at a seasonally adjusted 3.75 million annual rate, down 5.5 per cent on the month and 8.8 per cent on the year, and below the 3.85 million Reuters’ poll expected. The median price was $416,700, up just 0.4 per cent on a year earlier. Inventory rose to 1.59 million homes, 5.1 months of supply, the most since November 2015.

The mechanism is mortgage rates. Closings in October reflect contracts signed in August and September, when Freddie Mac’s 30-year rate passed 7 per cent and reached 7.40 — up 142 basis points since the war began in February. NAR’s Lawrence Yun called rates the highest since 2000, apart from one month in 2023. Thursday’s bond move (above) means relief is not coming in November.

Locally, the Suncoast Tampa Association of Realtors has Tampa Bay sales down about 5.2 per cent on a year ago. Some agents are telling out-of-state buyers to close before year-end: under Amendment 3, which raises the homestead exemption toward $250,000 over two years, later arrivals wait five years for the full exemption, the Tampa Bay Times reports.

Microsoft 365 is into a second day of a global outage that took down Outlook, Teams and Copilot

AI & tech industry New subject Primary source

Exchange Online, and with it Outlook, Teams and the Copilot assistant, has been failing since Thursday across regions. Microsoft’s status updates say recovery has been completed in parts of Asia-Pacific and is being extended elsewhere, mail delivery is catching up, and it has stretched the email timeout to 48 hours so delayed messages are not lost. It says it has no evidence of malicious activity and will publish a post-incident report.

Reach: Downdetector logged reports from 74 countries within an hour, per DW. Britain’s NHS reported serious impact, Deutsche Bahn struggled to send diverted-train timetables, and the Guardian’s live coverage lists UK police and schools. Microsoft at one point said its fixes were “not taking as expected.”

Why it leads: this is the single point of failure most US businesses and agencies run their mail through. If your organisation is on Microsoft 365, assume intermittent mail delays today.

Divergence — what actually broke?

What is disputed. A facts dispute over cause, still moving. Microsoft’s status page says servers unexpectedly became unavailable and a process that establishes secure connections was overwhelmed; it is also reverting a recent network change that correlated with the start. A university IT status page relaying Microsoft’s guidance said the issue was confined to the Microsoft 365 network gateway. The Guardian reports Microsoft pointing to server settings.

Why they differ. Timing: these are successive updates during an active incident, each relayed at a different hour. All three are compatible with a configuration change that tipped gateway servers into failure.

What it means for you. Believe “not a cyberattack, a change gone wrong” for now. The post-incident report settles it.

Four nonprofits have asked a San Francisco court to freeze frontier AI at five labs, the same week Anthropic’s IPO drew six times the orders

AI lab safety New subject Primary source

Public Citizen, the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Heat Initiative and the Alliance for Secure AI filed suit on Thursday in San Francisco Superior Court against Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceX’s AI unit. They seek an emergency injunction barring the companies from developing or deploying systems more capable than their current models, and from using AI to run AI research autonomously, until safeguards exist. The complaint leans on the companies’ own published warnings about bioweapons and cyberattack risk.

The legal odds are long: a state court ordering a halt to national research programmes would be without precedent, and no hearing date has been reported. The significance is political. Al Jazeera ties the filing to the Democratic midterm sweep, and Semafor reports AI safety groups pressing Democrats to step up oversight of the administration’s AI policy.

The contrast: Bloomberg reports Anthropic’s IPO, seeking up to $64.4 billion plus a $26 billion private placement, is about six times oversubscribed — potentially the largest listing ever, with existing backers prioritised.

Divergence — is this a suit to halt AI or to cap it?

What is disputed. A facts dispute about scope. Al Jazeera’s headline says the groups sued to “halt AI development.” The complaint and Public Citizen’s release ask for something narrower: no models beyond current capability and no autonomous AI research and development.

Why they differ. Headline compression, mainly. The narrower ask is also the one a court could conceivably grant, which is why the filers framed it that way.

What it means for you. Read it as a demand for a capability ceiling, not a shutdown. Products you use today would not be touched even if it succeeded. Whether a judge sets an injunction hearing will show how seriously the court takes it.

Trump threatened to double tariffs on Mexico immediately unless it stops a migrant caravan

Trade & tariffs New subject Independent corroboration

In a Truth Social post on Thursday, Trump said Mexico must stop the caravans “NOW” or tariffs would be “IMMEDIATELY DOUBLED.” He did not say which tariffs, whether USMCA exemptions would survive, or when. Today the main exposure is a 25 per cent tariff on goods that do not qualify under USMCA, plus steel duties under negotiation.

Stakes: Mexico is the largest US trading partner, with $96.34 billion of two-way trade in September alone, $38.9 billion of it through Laredo, per FreightWaves. Semafor notes the threat lands with Stephen Miller now elevated to chief of staff, which it reads as closing off any post-midterm softening on immigration.

For the reader: a doubled tariff on non-USMCA goods would hit autos, parts and produce first. Watch whether a formal order or Federal Register notice follows; the post alone changes nothing at the border.

Divergence — is there a caravan problem at all?

What is disputed. Facts. Trump describes a migrant influx. President Sheinbaum said at her Thursday briefing that no caravan has reached the northern border since January 2025, while acknowledging two are moving through southern Mexico. Sizes differ: Reuters put the group at about 1,500, EFE at roughly 2,000 at the outset.

Why they differ. Interest on both sides: the threat is leverage in live trade talks, and Sheinbaum must show she is in control. The size gap is timing, since caravans shed people as they move.

What it means for you. Sheinbaum’s record supports her on the border, and Semafor notes crossings have plummeted. The tariff risk is real but conditional; a caravan dispersed in southern Mexico, as before, would defuse it.

GAO says the F-35 programme cannot locate more than 17,000 parts worth up to $2.5 billion

Defense New subject Single-sourced

A new Government Accountability Office report finds that, as of March 2026, more than 17,000 F-35 parts worth about $1.3 billion were missing from the programme’s inventory system; officials told GAO the figure could reach $2.5 billion once missing cost data and acquisition expenses are counted. The cause is contractor records that are not routinely fed into the Pentagon’s system of record, across a supply chain of roughly 26 million parts at more than 700 sites and some 4,000 suppliers.

GAO did not link it to the separate Pentagon investigation into F-35 components shipped from Australia that ended up in Hong Kong, but the two together are a counterintelligence problem as much as an accounting one. It lands the week the Nvidia affair put technology leakage to China at the top of the agenda.

For the reader: this is the most expensive weapons programme in history, and the audit finding is the kind a Democratic House will use in oversight next year.

Florida: the Cabinet votes Tuesday on paying $20.5 million for 30 acres at the north tip of Clearwater Beach

Florida & Tampa Bay New subject Independent corroboration

The 17 November Cabinet agenda asks the Governor and Cabinet to spend $20.5 million of Florida Forever money on 30 acres at the northern end of Clearwater Beach Island, beside Caladesi Island State Park, including a 3,400-square-foot beachfront house. The Tampa Bay Times reports the package was listed at $37.5 million in 2024. The state’s case is access to Caladesi and a buffer for nearly 18 miles of protected barrier islands in the Pinellas County Aquatic Preserve, which a state report says development increasingly threatens.

The same agenda carries about $220 million for conservation easements on roughly 55,000 acres of farmland statewide, including $77.75 million for the Osceola Prairie ranch near Avon Park Air Force Range, with $15 million expected from the Pentagon.

Why it is here on local merit: it is a Pinellas beach, one county south of New Port Richey, and a yes vote takes that stretch of shoreline out of private development.
