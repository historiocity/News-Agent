The President rejected frontier-lab pacing; Altman spelled out a federal framework overnight

AI & tech industry New development Independent corroboration

Speaking in Ireland on Sunday, President Trump dismissed weekend calls from Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Elon Musk to “pace the frontier.” He said the United States is leading China on AI and that “whoever wins AI, wins,” while allowing that “we can put guardrails.” He characterised the catastrophic-risk warnings as coming from “negative forces… bringing up things that won’t happen.”

That is the White House answer to Saturday’s essay and posts. Overnight into Monday, Altman posted a longer note welcoming “a federal framework that sets consistent safety requirements for frontier AI,” repeating that pacing “does not mean stopping,” and saying OpenAI is excited by independent auditors. Beijing, via state and semi-official channels, hit back at Amodei’s China-coordination argument as an attempt to curb Chinese development — Amodei himself had called China the “toughest dilemma” for any slowdown.

What to watch: whether either lab names an evaluator and on what terms (standing access with publication rights, or project-scoped); Newsom’s 30 September decisions on SB 1000 and SB 947; any citation of the essay in DOJ preemption litigation.

This edition is written with an xAI model. Anthropic and OpenAI are subjects of this story; treat characterisations of either with ordinary scepticism.

Five §338 Canada proclamations landed in Monday’s Federal Register

US politics & policy World economy New subject Primary source

Monday’s Federal Register (Vol. 91) carries five presidential documents under the Section 338 Canada programme: three excluding certain Canadian products from importation (alcoholic beverages; dairy; motor vehicles / motorcycles) and two modifying the scope of products subject to the additional duties (alcoholic beverages; motor vehicles). Document numbers 2026-18835 through 2026-18839. These are the Register publications of the proclamations this brief has tracked since 8 September — not new authority invented overnight, but the formal FR drop.

Mechanics unchanged from prior issues: duty-list modifications bite Tuesday, 15 September; outright import bans on named alcohol, dairy and motorcycle/moped (>800cc) lines bite 29 September. Annexes are 8-digit HTS. Severability still sends struck bans back to the 50% duty, not duty-free. USMCA gives no relief (19 U.S.C. §4512(a)(1)). Canada’s ~US$20bn counter-tariff has been in force since 8 September.

What to watch: first CIT complaint naming §338; ITC investigation; preliminary injunction; further proclamations. Reader asked to track to judgment, not first filing.

Sources: FR JSON API, 14 Sep issue (primary listing) · FR docs 2026-18835–18839 · prior §338 chronology in this brief

The House is teeing up a bill that would make data centres pay for their own power

AI & tech industry Energy & grid US politics & policy New subject Underreported

H.R. 9340, the bipartisan Ratepayer Protection Act (Castor and others), is on the House schedule this week. The bill would require states to consider making large data centres — loads of at least 100 megawatts — cover the full incremental generation, transmission and distribution costs they create, including financial assurances. It is the legislative half of the fight this brief has tracked on the regulatory side through FERC’s six show-cause orders (RM26-4).

Why it clears the altitude bar: cost allocation is the part that reaches household bills through state rate cases. FERC’s interconnection orders left that largely open. A House bill that forces the question into state PUCs is how AI capex becomes a retail-power story with a statute attached.

What to watch: House floor action and any roll call; whether the 100 MW threshold survives markup; state PUC dockets that cite it even before enactment.

Sources: House docs this week — H.R. 9340 PDF · Congress.gov H.R. 9340 · Reuters, 10 Sep · FERC RM26-4 background in prior issues

The bank exam threshold doubled this morning — 188 institutions

US financials Primary source Follow-up

The OCC / Fed / FDIC interim final rule raising the consolidated-asset threshold for an 18-month on-site examination cycle from $3bn to under $6bn is effective today, Monday 14 September (FR 2026-18766). Comments close 14 October. It implements §903 of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act — supervision riding inside a housing bill.

Agencies estimate ~188 additional institutions qualify (including ~19 foreign-bank subsidiaries), bringing the extended-cycle population to about 4,016. Eligibility still requires well capitalised, well managed, composite CAMELS 1 or 2, no outstanding enforcement, no recent change of control. Still unpublished: aggregate assets of the 188 — that, not the count, says how much of the system moved to a lighter cadence.

What to watch: the asset total; critical comments by mid-October; a failure inside the newly lighter band.

Sources: FR 2026-18766 (primary) · prior Fed economic-analysis PDF · OCC joint release

Tomorrow, every student visa stops being open-ended

US politics & policy Primary source Follow-up

Weekday reminder

Effective date is Tuesday, 15 September 2026 — not Monday. Friday and Saturday Grok editions mislabelled the weekday; Sunday’s Issue 9-grok corrected it. Re-derived from the calendar again this morning.

DHS final rule FR 2026-14439 takes effect tomorrow. F, J and I classifications lose admission for “duration of status” and get a fixed admission period: programme end date on the I-20 or DS-2019, capped at four years, plus 30 days. Existing D/S holders may remain until programme end or four years from the effective date, whichever is first. After that, staying means filing an extension of stay.

NAFSA / Presidents’ Alliance / AFT sued in D. Mass.; argument was heard 9 September. No ruling and no stay as of this morning. Neither this rule nor the separate proposed deletion of the 60-day post-employment grace period (comments close 10 November) commits USCIS to any adjudication-time standard on extensions.

Same Tuesday: Canada §338 duty-list modifications bite (see above). Do not merge the two — different instruments, same calendar collision.
