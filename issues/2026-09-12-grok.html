Bab el-Mandeb traffic halved the day after Perim fell — the watchlist trigger that did not fire Friday

Geopolitics Energy & grid Follow-up Independent corroboration

Preliminary Kpler data, reported Saturday by The National and stood up by UA.NEWS, put Friday’s Bab el-Mandeb vessel transits at 15, down from 30 on Thursday — six exiting the Red Sea, nine entering. Cargoes included crude, grain and steel; three ships were in ballast. That is the named wake on this brief’s Bab el-Mandeb thread: a collapse in transit counts.

Friday’s EOD Grok edition said transit and underwriting markers had not moved and kept Bab below the line. That framing is now wrong, and saying so is the point of having named the trigger. The drop follows the Houthi seizure of Perim (Mayyun), Mocha and Dhubab — positions on both banks of the strait.

The pump did not wait for a Joint War Committee circular. AAA’s national diesel average as of 12 September is $6.1602 — the highest figure on AAA’s own recorded table, up from $6.0556 on Friday morning. Gasoline is $4.3104. Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline, the Hormuz workaround, still has no restart notice after Thursday’s precautionary shutdown. Brent settled Friday near $104.61, WTI near $100.05.

What to watch: whether Saturday and Sunday transits stay depressed or snap back; war-risk premiums or a JWC relisting (none found this morning); and any East-West restart before Monday’s Oman meeting.

Divergence — one-day blip, or effective control?

What is disputed. Not the Kpler preliminary count for Friday. Whether a single-day halving is the start of an underwriting-driven closure or a holiday/weekend artefact plus fear.

Why they differ. Chokepoint stories compress fast. Wire copy that led Perim as a “completed takeover” needs traffic to prove it; shipping desks wait for a week of AIS and a premium print. One Friday is a signal, not yet a series.

What it means for you. Hold the watchlist rule: premiums, JWC, sustained transit collapse, or a US kinetic response. Friday’s 15 is the first hard transit number since Perim. If the next three days stay near that level, the strait is effectively controlled for pricing even if lawyers say the water is open. Diesel at a new AAA record with East-West still dark is the household end of the same chain.

Dig deeper — why transit counts settle the argument the island seizure could not

Issue 7 led Perim and warned not to read occupation of the rocks as closure of the channel. The observation that would settle it was mundane: war-risk premiums and weekly transit counts. Friday EOD still lacked both. Saturday morning has the count.

Halving from 30 to 15 is not yet the −15% already baked into older Red Sea figures, and it is not a Joint War Committee relist. It is shipowners voting with AIS. Combined with East-West still offline and diesel setting another record on a softer crude tape, the products market is pricing refining tightness plus two impaired Middle East routes — not a headline barrel alone.

The US links Chinese satellite imagery to the July strike that killed three American troops

Geopolitics US politics & policy New subject Single-sourced

US officials familiar with the matter say that before Iran’s 17 July ballistic-missile strike on a housing area at Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan — three US troops killed, four wounded — Tehran acquired high-resolution satellite images of the base from Chinese entities. Iran procured images both before and after the attack. The reporting, in the Wall Street Journal and stood up Saturday by Hindustan Times, calls it the clearest evidence so far that Chinese support for Iran during the war had lethal consequences for US forces.

Officials declined to name the Chinese entities and did not allege direct involvement by Beijing. Senior US officials raised the imagery with Chinese counterparts, who demanded proof; Beijing has repeatedly dismissed the concern. In May the administration sanctioned MizarVision, Earth Eye and Chang Guang over imagery that could help Iran target US and partner forces. Chang Guang was previously accused of supporting Houthi targeting.

The finding lands twelve days before Xi Jinping is reported due at the White House on 24 September. Trump has told his team that maintaining relations with Xi is a priority; public US comments after July played the assistance down. Officials separately worry China could be helping Iran stalk US naval assets after recent near-miss ballistic shots at carriers.

What to watch: whether the administration puts the finding on the record before the 24th; any further designations; and whether the summit readout mentions Iran support at all.

Divergence — commercial imagery, or state help?

What is disputed. Not that a WSJ-sourced account exists. Whether “Chinese entities” means policy in Beijing or a sanctions-evasion market the Party tolerates.

Why they differ. US officials blur commercial and state on purpose because Chinese remote-sensing firms often sit under Party oversight. Beijing’s line treats any link as fabrication. Without named firms in the Saturday write-up beyond the May sanctions list, outsiders cannot adjudicate.

What it means for you. Label this single-sourced and hold it as summit furniture. The actionable channel is sanctions and export pressure the US can actually pull — not a courtroom finding. A White House or State on-record statement before the 24th would upgrade it; silence would mean the relationship priority won.

JPMorgan now sees a hike next week and another in December

Chief US economist Michael Feroli cited core PCE stuck above 3% each month this year and minimal progress toward the 2% target. The bank had previously leaned December-only.

The print that moved desks is already in Friday’s EOD edition and is not re-led here: headline CPI +0.4% m/m and +3.4% y/y; core +0.3% m/m (a tenth hot) and +2.4% y/y; food-at-home unchanged; transportation services +0.5%; gasoline +3.9% m/m; fuel oil +10.1% m/m and +52% y/y. FedWatch odds for a Wednesday hike sat in the mid-to-high 80s to nearly 90%. The funds rate is 3.50–3.75%. The Fed’s calendar has the meeting 15–16 September, statement and dots Wednesday the 16th at 2 p.m. Eastern. The 10-year touched near 4.98% before easing.

Your cadence on the Fed is prints and the decision only. This lead is the US-financials half: a systemically important bank rewriting its path after the last print before the meeting — not a state-of-play item on a quiet day.

What to watch: Wednesday’s statement and dots; whether dissenters from July are joined; and whether food-at-home stays flat in the next CPI while diesel sets records.

Florida & Tampa Bay: officials are investigating a possible dengue death — and the vaccine comment window closes Sunday

Florida & Tampa Bay Science & public health New subject Independent corroboration
Correction — Friday EOD miss

Friday’s EOD Grok edition said no severe or hospitalised case had been named. Local outlets had already reported that the Florida Department of Health is investigating a Hillsborough death possibly associated with dengue. That is the watchlist severe-case trigger. It should have run Friday evening; it leads the local section this morning.

DOH confirmed to WFTS/TampaBay28 that it is actively investigating the recently reported death of a Hillsborough resident possibly associated with dengue infection, and said it cannot provide further details under Florida Statutes §381.0031(6). Family accounts, carried by Patch and others, say a Tampa woman died on her 80th birthday at Tampa General Hospital. Cause is not officially confirmed.

The week-35 ledger from Friday stands: 111 locally acquired cases statewide, Hillsborough 95. TGH’s Kartik Cherabuddi said the mainland US has not seen this many local cases in the modern era, and that the hospital is seeing moderate and severe disease with hospitalisations. Hillsborough health officer Carla Fry noted only about one in four infections is symptomatic — so the true burden is higher than the ledger.

Separately, comment on school-entry vaccine rule 64D-3.046 closes Sunday 14 September. Physicians requested a hearing; none has been granted. If the window closes without one, the next fight is at DOAH. Pasco’s final budget adoption is Tuesday 22 September; Pinellas Thursday 24 September — dates corrected Friday.

What to watch: whether DOH confirms or rules out dengue as cause; week 36; Pasco reaching double figures; a granted vaccine hearing date.

On Monday, duration of status ends — the first of two defaults coming off

US politics & policy Where things stand Primary source Follow-up

The text did not move overnight. The calendar did: DHS final rule FR 2026-14439 takes effect Monday 15 September, ending duration-of-status admission for F, J and I classifications. Fixed admission to the I-20 / DS-2019 end date, capped at four years, plus 30 days. Existing D/S holders may remain to programme end or four years from the effective date, whichever is first.

The companion proposal to delete the post-employment grace period (FR 2026-18631) remains open for comment through 10 November. Two structural defaults off nonimmigrant status inside a month; Monday is the first bite. Status can be cured; unlawful presence triggers the non-discretionary 3- and 10-year bars.

What to watch: any last-minute stay; extension-of-stay adjudication times; who files on the grace-period docket.

Sources: FR 2026-14439 · FR 2026-18631 · 10b / 11 / 11b-grok framings
