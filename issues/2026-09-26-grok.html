SCOTUS stayed the Eighth Circuit again: Missouri runs November on the 2022 map

In a per curiam stay dated 25 Sep, the Supreme Court granted the application in No. 26A388 and stayed: the Eighth Circuit’s 21 Sep mandate and 22 Sep order in No. 26–2797, and the Eastern District of Missouri’s 21 and 22 Sep injunctions (ECF 57 and 60). The Court’s own 10 Sep stay remains in effect. Operative instruction to the lower courts: do not enjoin use of the 2022 map, and do not order Missouri to use the 2025 map, for the 2026 congressional election.

State-law baseline (Missouri Supreme Court, von Glahn v. Hoskins, 3 Sep): because a sufficient referendum petition attached to HB 1, the 2025 map “is not the law and has never been the law” until voters approve it in November. Federal plaintiffs then argued the U.S. Constitution required the 2025 map anyway — after the August primary ran on it. Absentee and military ballots on the 2022 map are already in the mail; county clerks told the Eighth Circuit it is “practically impossible” to revert. The Court leaned on Purcell as well as its own prior stays.

What to watch: the November referendum on HB 1 itself; any further emergency application; whether the Eighth Circuit merits track continues under the stay.

Sources: SCOTUS per curiam 26A388 (PDF) · Missouri Supreme Court von Glahn (3 Sep) via State Court Report · text.npr.org headline · Al Jazeera election desk

SCOTUS revived DHS’s modified SAVE citizenship-check tool — with a hard November limit

Same afternoon, in No. 26A308 (Department of Homeland Security v. League of Women Voters), the Court stayed a D.C. district injunction that had blocked DHS’s 2025 expansion of the Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (SAVE) program for voter-roll checks. The modified SAVE adds Social Security records and bulk searches so states can query citizenship status of registered voters — the March 2025 EO 14248 build-out.

Substance for November is narrower than the headline. The National Voter Registration Act’s 90-day quiet period before a federal election still bars systematic voter-roll purges. Votebeat’s read (election-administration desk): individualized checks can resume now; bulk post-election scrutiny can restart after 3 Nov; mass pre-election removals should not. Jackson, joined by Sotomayor and Kagan, dissented, citing false positives (including Texas cases where citizens were flagged).

Do not confuse SAVE-the-database with the SAVE Act / SAVE America Act citizenship-proof bills, which have not become law. Earlier this month the Court also denied the USPS mail-ballot stay for November — so two election-administration fights resolved in opposite directions inside two weeks.

What to watch: which states restart bulk queries after Election Day; whether the D.C. Circuit merits panel restores the injunction; documented false-positive rates in the next state audits.

Sources: SCOTUS per curiam 26A308 (PDF) · Votebeat · SCOTUSblog · text.npr.org · Al Jazeera

Sixth Circuit: Ohio and Tennessee may regulate Kalshi sports contracts as gambling

A unanimous Sixth Circuit panel Friday held that Kalshi had not shown its sports-event contracts are CFTC “swaps” under the Commodity Exchange Act, and — even assuming they were — that the CEA does not expressly or impliedly preempt Ohio’s or Tennessee’s gambling laws. Result: affirm the Southern District of Ohio’s denial of a preliminary injunction; vacate the Middle District of Tennessee’s injunction; remand. Follows a Ninth Circuit loss for platforms on Nevada; breaks from the Third Circuit’s April New Jersey ruling favouring exclusive CFTC jurisdiction.

What to watch: cert petition / New Jersey’s existing Supreme Court ask; whether other AGs copy the Ohio/Tennessee posture; any CFTC interpretive release.
