One chain: seven unpublished Hormuz conditions, Iraq’s 2m barrels, AAA’s twelfth diesel fall, another Sunday tanker hit

Energy & grid Geopolitics Trigger fired Primary / wire

One causal chain, not four stories. Parliament speaker Mohammad Baqer Ghalibaf said Sunday the Strait of Hormuz “will not be opened” until Iran’s seven conditions based on the Islamabad memorandum of understanding are realised. Foreign minister Abbas Araghchi has already put a clock on the other side: the strait could reopen within seven days if the conditions are accepted — “The choice now is with the US.” The memorandum is dated 17 June, described as a Pakistani- and Qatari-mediated framework for durable peace, and has never been published. Qatar has been carrying messages between Araghchi and US envoys Witkoff and Kushner (New York, September); Tehran says Washington’s reply came back through the Qataris, and no reachable account says what it was.

No source publishes a numbered list of the seven. Two partial accounts together name seven distinct demands — this enumeration is assembled by this brief, not quoted from a document: end the fighting on all fronts; return frozen funds; lift the US naval blockade of Iranian ports; exempt Iranian oil exports from sanctions; stop interfering in internal affairs; stop cooperating with Iran’s adversaries; halt all attacks on Iranian territory. Items one and seven may be one condition counted twice. Four of those are permanent US policy changes traded for a reversible restoration of shipping — the structure of the ask, not its contents, is what decides whether it can be accepted. Coordinates of the restricted zone remain unpublished (26 days).

Then the fact that cuts across the declarations. Iraq’s state Oil Tankers Company reported moving two million barrels of Iraqi crude through the strait on Saturday aboard a VLCC — its first such operation in decades, with Iranian permission previously secured for Iraqi tankers. Separately, UKMTO said Sunday a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in the strait, engine-room damage, crew safe, no environmental impact reported. “Closed” is a claim about permission, not about empty water: a licensing regime with a shooting enforcement mechanism, through which cargoes are moving.

At the US pump the daily card is still falling from the 22 Sep record. AAA’s Sunday national diesel average is $6.3434 (−1.20¢ from Saturday’s $6.3554) — the twelfth consecutive daily decline — with regular at $4.3697. That is the level. For trend, EIA’s weekly all-grades / on-highway table remains the instrument (Tuesday cycle). The G7/IEA 100-million-barrel release with diesel front-loaded in the first 20 days (Friday) is a one- to four-month overlay, not a substitute for chokepoint volumes. Brent held near ~$100–102 on reachable tickers into the weekend — name the feed; do not treat screen Brent as Dated physical while freight is dislocated.

What to watch: publication of the memorandum or a numbered list; a US answer not relayed through Qatar; Hormuz transit counts (still the missing official series); AAA’s next daily against the EIA weekly; any Federal Register / BIS instrument if an export measure returns.

Divergence — is the strait closed?

What is disputed. Ghalibaf: it will not be opened until conditions are met. Iraq’s tanker company: 2m barrels moved through it Saturday. Both can be true if “closed” means a permission regime rather than an absence of hulls.

Why they differ. Iran is asserting authority over a waterway whose legal status it disputes (signed but never ratified UNCLOS). Iraq has to sell oil. Markets price both the declaration and the throughput — which is why the pump can fall while the rhetoric hardens.

What it means for you. Read volumes and the AAA card, not slogans. Four permanent US concessions for a reversible reopen is the deal structure to watch. Do not call a diesel trend turn off the daily series alone (22 Sep standing rule).

Florida AG asks a Highlands County judge to bar OpenAI from new models — and from ChatGPT for Florida minors

AI & tech industry Lab safety Florida & Tampa Bay New subject Underreported

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier has filed a ~38–49-page motion for a temporary injunction in Highlands County Circuit Court (10th Judicial Circuit; case reported as 26000295GCAXMX) asking Judge Heather Beato to restrict OpenAI while the state’s June consumer-protection suit proceeds. The ask, as reported: (1) bar OpenAI from developing new AI models unless those models are subject to independent third-party safety guardrails and approval; (2) bar offering ChatGPT to minors in Florida; (3) bar collecting or processing personal information from children under 13 without COPPA / Florida parental-consent compliance.

No judge has ruled. No hearing date is announced. Nothing changes for Florida ChatGPT users today. OpenAI’s public line (via spokespeople to Axios and others) is that it wants “pragmatic AI policies that apply to the entire AI industry — not just one company.” The motion cites a 26 Sep Axios account of tens of thousands of security incidents at OpenAI and Anthropic — a news report in a court filing, not a court finding; Anthropic is not a party.

This is the standing state AI legislation / lab-safety thread with a Florida local bar: a state AG seeking to pause frontier model development from a rural circuit court is an aggressive jurisdictional ask that OpenAI is expected to contest (personal jurisdiction, extraterritorial reach, First Amendment, possible removal). Supreme Court term opens Monday with Suncor v. Boulder — federal preemption of state climate torts; Alito recused

US politics & policy Energy & grid New subject

The October 2026 term opens Monday 5 October (first Monday in October). First argument: Suncor Energy Inc. v. County Commissioners of Boulder County (No. 25-170) — whether federal law precludes state-law claims for injuries allegedly caused by interstate and international greenhouse-gas emissions, and whether the Court has statutory / Article III jurisdiction to hear the case. Boulder County and the City of Boulder sued Suncor entities and Exxon Mobil under Colorado tort and consumer-protection theories seeking millions for climate-related local harms. The Colorado Supreme Court let the claims proceed; the companies sought cert.

The mechanism is the story: a preemption ruling would dispose of roughly two dozen similar city/county/state climate tort suits wholesale; a loss for the companies opens discovery in state courts nationwide. Justice Alito announced 28 Sep he will not participate (oil-and-gas stock holdings), creating a possible 4–4 tie that would leave the Colorado decision in place. Cornell LII’s argument preview frames the Clean Air Act field/conflict fight and the finality/jurisdiction fight in full — primary-adjacent for a Sunday when the Court’s own calendar PDF cannot be parsed here.

What to watch: Monday’s questioning — preemption doctrine vs climate merits; whether justices treat the CAA as occupying the field or look for express preemption; the ICE mandatory-detention case also on the docket (Issue 28).

Clocks almost certainly fall back 1 November: Senate adjourned until ~9 Nov; permanent DST still isn’t permanent standard time

US politics & policy New subject

The House passed the Sunshine Protection Act (H.R. 139) in July, 308–117, making daylight saving time permanent — not standard time. Trump spent the weekend pressing Senate Republicans, naming Sen. Tom Cotton as the holdup and publishing a number he urged Arkansans to call. Majority Leader John Thune has questioned whether the bill can reach 60 votes. Cotton’s objection is substantive: permanent DST darkens winter mornings (school and commute before dawn), which is the same fact as lighter evenings stated twice.

The calendar fact that reshapes the story: reporting has the Senate adjourned for the midterms and not due back until the week of 9 November — after clocks fall back on Sunday 1 November. Absent an extraordinary return, this fall’s switch happens regardless of whether the bill later passes. States may already sit on permanent standard time (Arizona, Hawaii); federal law forbids a state choosing permanent daylight time alone — so the Act is a national answer to a question states are only half-allowed to settle.

What to watch: any surprise floor action before 1 Nov; a permanent-standard-time amendment that would reframe the coalition; Florida’s prior endorsement of permanent DST (latitude-tolerant) vs northern Senate geography.
