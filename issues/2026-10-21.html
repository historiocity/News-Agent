Diesel turned back up, and the reason is now visible: stocks are at the lowest of the autumn

Energy & grid Continuation Primary source

The weekly reading this brief has named as decisive in four consecutive issues published overnight, and it broke the way the daily series said it would. EIA's weekly on-highway diesel average for the week ending 19 October is $6.276, up 9.7 cents — the first weekly rise since the $6.529 record of 21 September, and the end of a two-week decline through $6.199 and $6.179.

Petrol went the other way. EIA's weekly regular average is $4.284, down 4.7 cents, a third consecutive fall from $4.354 and $4.331. The official series now says what the daily card average has said for a week: the two fuels have come apart. On EIA's numbers the gap is $1.992; on AAA's this morning — diesel $6.3174, regular $4.2644 — $2.0530, a fresh widest.

Now the part this brief could not previously supply. US distillate stocks for the week ending 9 October stand at 102.6 million barrels, down 2.5 million in a week and 5.2 million below the 11 September peak of 107.9 million. It is the lowest reading since at least early August. Refinery utilisation is 93.5 per cent, recovered from a 92.5 per cent trough on 25 September but well under the 97.8 per cent of 4 September.

That is an ordinary explanation, worth saying plainly because this brief has reported the diesel price for six weeks without being able to give one. Refineries went into autumn maintenance and runs fell more than five points; distillate drew down through late September; the product that draws hardest turned up while petrol, whose demand falls seasonally, kept easing. No chokepoint, sanctions decision or refinery strike is needed to account for this week's move. Those matter for the level; the turn is a stock draw.

What to watch: the week-ending-16-October stocks figure, published with today's weekly petroleum report, which will say whether the draw continued; utilisation climbing back toward 96 per cent, which is when maintenance season ends; and whether the premium holds above $2.00 on the weekly series as well as the daily one.

Confidence: Primary source throughout. Diesel and petrol weekly averages, distillate stocks and refinery utilisation were each read from EIA's own weekly series; the AAA figures are from AAA this morning; the gaps are this brief's arithmetic. The 16 October stocks reading is not yet published and the figures above end 9 October, a ten-day lag that matters when the price moved last week. Nearly two in five people on the Justice Department's noncitizen list said they were born here

US politics & policy New thread Independent corroboration

Yesterday this brief put the Justice Department's California voting prosecutions below the line because it could not read the underlying documents. Court filings made on Tuesday supply what was missing, and they are about the list rather than the charges.

The Department has demanded that California segregate the ballots of roughly 20,000 people it says are "likely noncitizens" registered to vote in the state, on data from Homeland Security investigators, and has sued to compel the state to investigate them before their votes are counted. The list was first delivered to Los Angeles County on a thumb drive, which county officials declined to insert into a government computer; the Department emailed a spreadsheet the next day.

Los Angeles County's registrar then checked it. Of the 20,000 names, only 7,035 had county addresses, and some were duplicates. Officials sampled 200. Assistant Registrar-Recorder Monica Flores wrote that the review is preliminary but "it indicates that the October List is unreliable" — and that of those whose birthplace was meaningfully reported, 38.9 per cent indicated they were born in the United States, which she called strong evidence of citizenship.

California's answer is that complying this close to the election is unfeasible and would likely breach federal and state election law, and one filing calls the suit "a reckless political stunt" that threatens to disenfranchise thousands. The election is 13 days away.

Two cautions on the 38.9 per cent, because it is the figure that will travel. It comes from a sample of 200, not the full list, and covers only records where birthplace was "meaningfully reported" — California asks for place of birth at online registration but does not require it, so the denominator is unstated. It supports the claim that the list is unreliable; it does not yet support an estimate of how many of the 20,000 are citizens.

What to watch: the full county review rather than the 200-record sample; whether the court orders segregation before 3 November; and whether the other counties holding the remaining 13,000 names publish their own checks.

Confidence: Independent corroboration. The figures, the quoted words and the thumb-drive sequence are from court filings made on Tuesday by the State of California and Los Angeles County officials, as reported by NPR; this brief read the reporting, not the filings, and has not seen the Department's complaint or the DHS methodology behind the list. The 28 criminal charges reported yesterday are a separate matter and no charging document has been read. Sources: NPR · 20 October

Nigeria has a case off a flight from Uganda, and the US order still names three countries

Science & public health US politics & policy Continuation Single-sourced

Nigeria's Health Ministry says a woman who arrived on a flight from Uganda has tested positive — the country's first case in this outbreak. Kenya, meanwhile, has confirmed a ninth.

The route is the whole story. Uganda is one of the three countries named in the CDC order under 42 U.S.C. 265 that suspends entry into the United States of people travelling from there, along with the Democratic Republic of the Congo and South Sudan. Nigeria is not named. A traveller has now moved by air from a listed country to an unlisted one and tested positive on arrival — which is the mechanism the order is written to interrupt, operating one step outside its reach.

Nigeria is not Kenya for this purpose. Lagos and Abuja carry direct scheduled service to the United States and Nigeria has the largest population in Africa, so an unlisted country with a confirmed imported case is a materially different exposure from the one the order was drawn against three weeks ago.

The pattern now runs: DRC, then Uganda, then Kenya on 6 October with an index patient who crossed into Uganda by road and flew to Nairobi, then Nigeria by air from Uganda. Each step has gone through a country the previous order did not anticipate. The strain remains Bundibugyo, for which there is no licensed vaccine and no targeted treatment, and the WHO declared the outbreak a public health emergency of international concern in May.

What to watch: the 10 November expiry of the CDC order, when the country list is set again and when Nigeria and Kenya are the obvious questions; Nigeria's contact tracing, where the comparison with Kenya's 98.5 per cent is the benchmark; and whether any case appears outside a traced contact group in either country.

Confidence: Single-sourced. The Nigerian case is from Al Jazeera's index, attributed there to Nigeria's Health Ministry; this brief could not resolve the full article and did not reach the ministry statement, so the patient's condition, the airport and the contact count are unknown here. A search for corroboration returned Nigeria's own disease-control centre saying on 6 October that it had no confirmed case — consistent with a new case, not a contradiction, but it is not corroboration. The same search surfaced a report that France has a case in a doctor returning from the DRC, which this brief could not date and is not relying on. The CDC order's terms are primary, read on 15 October. Sources: Al Jazeera · Al Jazeera on Kenya's ninth · Nigeria CDC advisory · 18 October

The FAA cleared the 737 Max 10, nine years and 1,555 undelivered orders later

US financials AI & tech industry New thread Independent corroboration

The Federal Aviation Administration certified Boeing's 737 Max 10 for passenger service on Tuesday, nearly a decade after the aircraft was unveiled at the 2017 Paris air show. With certification, Boeing can sell and deliver it to domestic and foreign carriers.

The number that measures what this unlocks: more than 30 customers have ordered 1,555 Max 10s that Boeing has not delivered. That is an order book built over nine years and held up by regulators, and it is now releasable — subject to Boeing actually building the aircraft, which has been the other half of its problem.

The Max 10 is the stretch: 185–210 seats against the Max 9's 175–195 and the Max 8's 160–180. It exists to meet the top of Airbus's A321neo range, where Boeing has been losing the narrow-body contest, and the economics for airlines are seats per flight rather than anything new in the airframe.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy announced it at Boeing's Everett plant, saying certification followed extensive safety reviews including thousands of hours of test flights, and calling it a win for American manufacturing. Boeing Commercial Airplanes president and chief executive Stephanie Pope said it "unlocks a key to our future growth."

The context is not decoration. Two Max 8 crashes killed 346 people in 2018 and 2019; a door plug departed an Alaska Airlines Max 9 in flight in 2024. The regulator that delayed the Max 10 is the one that has just approved it — the ordinary way certification works, and the reason the approval will be read politically. What would make this a safety story rather than a commercial one is the content of the reviews, and neither the certification basis nor any special conditions were available here.

What to watch: the first delivery and to whom; the production rate, since the FAA's cap on 737 output after the 2024 door plug is the binding constraint now, not certification; and whether any airline discloses a changed order.

Confidence: Independent corroboration for the certification, the seat counts, the order backlog and the quoted officials, from NPR's report of Tuesday's announcement at Everett. The FAA's certification documents, the type certificate data sheet and any special conditions were not read, and the current status of the production-rate cap was not verified today. Sources: NPR

US politics & policy US debt & fiscal Continuation Primary source

They match. Section 4(a) waives the duties in sections 112 and 113; 4(b) the sanctions and restrictions in section 102(d), preserving measures under the Russia emergency continued on 24 March 2026 (91 FR 15515) as in effect the day before enactment; 4(c) sections 102(a), 102(e), 103, 104, 105, 106, 107, 108, 109, 110, 111 and 117; and 4(d) holds the waivers in effect until the President or the Secretary of State determines that further action is appropriate. No expiry, every operative section, the waiver power delegated onward. The section 115(b) certification and report are referenced four times and remain unpublished.

That verification is worth more than the restatement. This brief has twice built leads on documents read before their publication date — Monday's issue was largely written from Monday's Federal Register on the Saturday — and has each time told the reader the text could in principle change. It has now been checked against the published version and did not change.

Treasury's Monday print had the long end up six basis points; Tuesday's gives back most of it — ten-year 5.12, twenty-year 5.47, thirty-year 5.42 — while the two-year rose to 4.79. The consequence is that the twenty-minus-ten hump narrowed to +35 basis points, after fifteen consecutive sessions at +38. This brief has called that hump its most durable observation; this is the first day it has moved.

What to watch: whether the hump returns to +38 or stays narrower, which is the difference between noise and the first change in the curve's shape this month; and the certification, still the only document that would explain the waiver.

US financials The IRS has partially withdrawn its spin-off guidance, which is a bigger move than it sounds.

Treasury and the IRS published a partial withdrawal of proposed regulations under section 355, on the "device" and "active trade or business" requirements. Section 355 is the provision that lets a company spin off a subsidiary to shareholders without triggering corporate-level tax, and the device and active-business tests are the two gates it has to pass.

Withdrawing part of a proposal is how Treasury retreats without conceding. The device test asks whether a spin-off is really a disguised distribution of earnings; the active-business test asks whether both pieces are real operating businesses. Guidance tightening either one changes the arithmetic on every corporate separation being planned, and the tax-structuring bar reads these documents the day they appear.

Which paragraphs were withdrawn, and why, this brief does not know — the document was identified in the day's list and not read. Given how much corporate activity turns on section 355, it is the most consequential unread document in this issue.

Below the line because it is named rather than read, and a partial withdrawal means nothing until you know which part.

Energy & grid A month of "we cannot read this" turned out to be a month of not looking for the series.

Since 22 September this brief's configuration has carried a note that distillate inventories and refinery utilisation — the two numbers that would show whether diesel relief was coming — sat inside EIA's Weekly Petroleum Status Report, a PDF it could not parse, and were therefore unavailable every Wednesday. The note was repeated, and the gap was reported to the reader in several issues as a limitation.

It was wrong. Both series are on the same LeafHandler.ashx endpoint this brief has used for the weekly diesel price since September: distillate stocks at series WDISTUS1, refinery utilisation at WPULEUS3, each returning years of weekly history as plain HTML. They were never only in the PDF.

The error is worth naming precisely because it is a common one: a document failing to parse became "the data is unreachable", and nobody tested the second claim. The rule now recorded is to search the data provider's own series catalogue before declaring any number unavailable — and to re-test standing "unreachable" notes rather than inheriting them.

Below the line because it is this brief's own method rather than news, and the result of fixing it is the first story in this issue.
