Alberta voted 77 to 23 to stay in Canada, and the margin is a message to Washington

Geopolitics Energy & grid New thread Independent corroboration

With more than a million votes counted, 77 percent of Albertans voted to remain in Canada and 23 percent to begin a process toward separation, on unofficial results from Elections Alberta. The ballot was not a vote on independence: approval would have started Alberta down a path toward a future separation vote.

Premier Danielle Smith called the referendum under pressure from separatists inside her own United Conservative Party while opposing independence herself, and welcomed the result: "Albertans have voted for a stronger Alberta in a united Canada." Prime Minister Mark Carney said Albertans "chose unity" and tied the result directly to what comes next — accelerating trade diversification, major infrastructure, and making Canada an energy power with Alberta at the centre.

The American channel runs two ways. Alberta holds the world's fourth-largest proven oil reserves and sells almost all its crude to the United States, into Midwest refineries built for heavy grades with no alternative supplier. A constitutional crisis there would have been an American energy problem whoever won.

The second way is the one the reporting makes explicit. The result is described as a boost to Carney's unity push amid the US trade war and annexation talk — which is to say that an American posture intended to apply pressure to Ottawa appears instead to have consolidated Canadian opinion against separation. Whether that was the cause cannot be established from a referendum result, but the direction is not in doubt.

The dissent is still a quarter of the province. Keith Wilson, a separatist leader: "at least a quarter of Albertans feel that Alberta needs to be treated better." Results were delayed after late openings forced more than 100 polling stations to extend hours.

What to watch: Smith's address to the province, promised for Tuesday evening, which is where the 23 percent gets answered or ignored; whether Carney's diversification promise produces any pipeline or port decision that reduces the share of Albertan crude going south; and whether the UCP's internal separatist bloc accepts the number.

Confidence: Independent corroboration. Vote shares are unofficial results from Elections Alberta as reported by NPR from Edmonton; the quotations are from Carney, Smith, Wilson and former deputy premier Thomas Lukaszuk. Elections Alberta's own count was not read at source and the result is not final. The reserves ranking and the refinery point are context, not from today's reporting. Saudi Arabia called off its investment conference in the week it bought $9bn of American air defence

World economy US financials Continuation Independent corroboration

Saudi Arabia has postponed the Future Investment Initiative — the conference known as Davos in the Desert — from 26–29 October to a date in 2027 to be announced. Government staff have been told to work from home. The statement went up on the FII's own website on Tuesday.

The official reason is not the real one and the gap is unusually wide. FII Institute chief executive Dr Maha Bint Mishari Al Saud said the decision "comes from a deep commitment to our partners" and that because this is the landmark tenth edition the institute wants to ensure it delivers full scale and quality. The operative fact is in the same reporting: Houthi strikes on the kingdom are continuing, and on 8 October the Houthis said they attacked King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, with video showing smoke and flames.

What the conference is for measures what has been lost. FII exists to raise the foreign capital Vision 2030 needs to move the economy off hydrocarbons into tourism, sport and technology. Expected speakers included Alphabet and Google president Ruth Porat, Goldman Sachs chief executive David Solomon and BlackRock chief executive Laurence Fink — a fair proxy for the money the kingdom is courting, and the kingdom has decided it cannot guarantee their safety in Riyadh in six days.

Set that beside yesterday's lead. In the same week, the Defense Security Cooperation Agency notified Congress of roughly $9bn of new American weapons for Saudi Arabia, most of it air and missile defence: 112 additional THAAD interceptors and 15 launchers, and about ten thousand precision bomb kits. A country buying interceptors at that rate and cancelling its showcase conference is telling you two consistent things, and the second is the more candid.

What to watch: whether the 2027 date is ever set; whether other Gulf events move; and the insurance and airline response, which is the measure this brief named on 11 October and which produced Delta's Riyadh postponement.

The State Department has waived the child-soldier rules for eight countries, Russia among them

Geopolitics US politics & policy New thread Underreported Primary source

The Child Soldiers Prevention Act of 2008 bars American military assistance, training and arms-sales licences to governments identified as recruiting or using child soldiers; section 404 lets the President waive that bar in the national interest. This year's waiver published this morning as Public Notice 13150, signed by Secretary of State Marco Rubio on 30 September. It is close to comprehensive.

Full waivers of the section 404(a) prohibition for Ethiopia, Nigeria and Yemen. Partial waivers for the Central African Republic (military education and training, excess defence articles, direct commercial sales licences), the Democratic Republic of the Congo (training, peacekeeping assistance, DCS licences), Somalia (peacekeeping assistance and support under 10 U.S.C. 331 and 333), and Venezuela (military education and training). Then a general waiver allowing direct commercial sales licences tied to other US assistance for all of them.

And one standing apart: a waiver for the Russian Federation, "solely for the issuance of DCS licences in connection with the International Space Station."

The certification is a single sentence and it is the part that does the work: "I certify that the governments of the above countries are taking effective and continuing steps to address the problem of child soldiers." No evidence appears in the notice. The Memorandum of Justification that would contain it goes to Congress and is not published.

Two things belong together. Venezuela gets military training waived in the month the United States is running a pressure campaign in the region and has just issued an earthquake relief licence for Caracas. And Russia is on the American child-soldiers list at all — which the waiver discloses in passing — while being waived to keep the space station supplied, four days after the same administration waived the Graham Act sanctions.

What to watch: whether the Memorandum of Justification reaches the public, which is the only way the certification can be tested; and whether any of the eight sees an actual increase in US assistance, which is what the waiver permits rather than requires.

Medicare proposes to sever what pharmacy middlemen earn from what drugs cost

US politics & policy US financials New thread Underreported Primary source

A proposed rule from the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, file code CMS-4214-P, implements three sections of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026. One of them would change how a $200bn-a-year business is paid.

Section 6224 requires that a pharmacy benefit manager's compensation in Medicare Part D take the form of bona fide service fees that may not be tied to the price of a drug, or to the rebates, discounts and other price concessions the PBM negotiates. That is the central conflict in the industry stated as a rule: today a middleman paid a share of the rebate earns more when the list price is higher, which is the mechanism everyone has complained about for a decade.

It also imposes annual reporting to Part D sponsors and the Secretary — drug prices and utilisation, direct and indirect remuneration, formulary placement of branded against generic drugs, payment rates at affiliated versus unaffiliated pharmacies, and information on affiliates and brokers. The affiliated-versus-unaffiliated line matters most: it is how self-dealing between a PBM and the pharmacies it owns becomes visible.

CMS sets out the FTC's findings as background: spread pricing, where PBMs keep part of what a plan pays rather than passing it to the pharmacy on specialty generics; and PBM group purchasing organisations — rebate-negotiating spin-offs, three of them, one for each of the three largest PBMs — which the FTC said were likely formed to use the anti-kickback safe harbour written for real purchasing groups.

The other two sections: 6219 adjusts Part D cost-sharing reductions for low-income beneficiaries, and 6220, the REAL Health Providers Act, requires accurate provider directories — the fix for networks that list doctors who are not taking patients. Comments close 15 December 2026.

What to watch: what the final rule counts as a bona fide service fee, because the definition is the whole rule; whether the three PBM group purchasing organisations are addressed directly or only reported on; and whether the written agreements between sponsors and PBMs are made available to anyone outside the parties.

Confidence: Primary source. The statutory sections, the compensation restriction, the reporting list and the FTC background were read from the proposed rule itself, from GovInfo's PDF. The rule runs to several hundred thousand characters and this brief read the summary and the section 6224 discussion, not the whole document; the regulatory impact analysis and the cost-sharing provisions were not read. The $200bn figure is general context, not from the rule. Sources: CMS-4214-P · docket CMS-2026-2533

A state arrest warrant for Raúl Castro, thirty years on

Florida & Tampa Bay US politics & policy New thread Independent corroboration

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier announced on Monday that the state has issued an arrest warrant for Raúl Castro, 95, on state charges of conspiracy to commit first-degree murder. The charges concern his alleged role in ordering the shooting down of two unarmed civilian aircraft, in which four people were killed.

The aircraft belonged to Brothers to the Rescue, the Florida-based group that flew search patterns over the Straits looking for Cubans leaving the island by raft. Uthmeier named the dead: Carlos Costa, Pablo Morales, Mario de la Peña and a fourth. "Raúl Castro ordered the murder of four Floridians in cold blood," he said, "and Florida will never stop pursuing accountability."

The state action mirrors a federal indictment from May, and was announced in Miami alongside the US Attorney for the Southern District of Florida, Jason Reding Quiñones. Two prosecutions of the same 95-year-old man, in the same city, by two sovereigns, for the same deaths thirty years ago.

On what it will achieve, the honest answer is nothing directly. Castro is in Cuba, there is no extradition relationship, and he will not stand trial in Florida. The warrant is an instrument of pressure and of memory rather than of prosecution, and the timing — a state charge layered on a federal one as Washington escalates against Havana — makes the first of those explicit. That does not make it empty: it forecloses travel, it creates a standing legal fact, and for the families it is the first state-level acknowledgement in three decades.

What to watch: whether Havana responds formally, which it did to the May indictment; whether any co-defendant named in the state charges is reachable outside Cuba; and whether this becomes a template for other state-level charges against foreign officials.

US politics & policy Mahmoud Khalil has taken his deportation case to the Supreme Court.

Mahmoud Khalil has taken his deportation fight to the Supreme Court. The case has run since early 2025 and turns on whether a lawful permanent resident can be removed for speech — specifically, on the Secretary of State's authority to find that a non-citizen's presence has potentially serious adverse foreign policy consequences.

That provision has been used rarely and almost never against a green-card holder for political advocacy, which is why the case matters beyond its facts: a ruling either way sets the boundary of the foreign-policy removal ground for everyone holding permanent residence.

As with the admissions case above, this brief has the fact of the filing and not the filing — no petition, no question presented, no indication whether the Court has been asked for emergency relief or ordinary review, which are very different postures. AI & tech industry A prosecutor says the ex-OpenAI employee accused of planting a device had Saudi backing.

A prosecutor has alleged that the former OpenAI employee accused of planting a listening device had Saudi backing. On 16 October this brief noted the same case below the line, when the reporting was that the suspect had booked a one-way trip to Saudi Arabia.

A one-way ticket is circumstantial; an allegation of state backing is a different claim, and if it holds it makes this an industrial-espionage case involving a foreign government and a US frontier AI lab rather than a disgruntled-employee case. That is a materially larger story.
