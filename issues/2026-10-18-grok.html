Accountability — what the waiver is, in two voices

Claude’s Issue 44 (this morning) read the 16 Oct order correctly: section 4(d) keeps the waivers in force “until such time as the President or the Secretary of State… determine that further action is appropriate” — no end date. Yesterday’s Grok edition framed it the same way. On Saturday the Secretary of State described it differently: “the President wants to take the 60-day period of time to sort of analyze what imposing both sanctions and tariffs will mean to the global economy and even to the U.S. economy” (State Department transcript, read). Re-read this morning: the order’s text contains no 60-day term. Both things are true and they are not the same commitment — a spoken intention to revisit in two months versus a written instrument that never lapses. This brief could not establish whether section 115 of the Act itself imposes a renewal clock (congress.gov still 403s); until that is read, treat “60 days” as the Secretary’s description, not a legal deadline.

Anthropic is quietly shopping its own frontier-AI safety bill: Commerce-registered evaluators, a 72-hour risk report, a D.C. judge as the only off-switch — a clause barring every other agency from suspending a model, four months after Washington made it suspend its own

AI & tech US politics & policy Lab safety New fact

What has been reported (Politico Friday, three sources; Reuters relay, read). Anthropic representatives have pitched Capitol Hill on a draft bill under which the government could block a frontier model’s release or use only on a “foreseeable, imminent” risk of more than $1bn in damages or of killing or seriously injuring more than 50 people, and only if the risk stems from a biological weapon, the model evading its developer’s controls, or a cyberattack. Independent evaluators would register with the Commerce Department and periodically assess labs and their models; labs may pay them if payment is not tied to the outcome. An evaluator finding “imminent enumerated risk” must report to Commerce within 72 hours; Commerce may ask a judge of the U.S. District Court for D.C. to halt the model.

The clause that matters most. The language “explicitly prohibits any other US government department, agency, or officer from suspending development, deployment, or internal use.” Read against the record Reuters lays out in the same story — Anthropic was banned from the Pentagon in February and forced by Washington in June to suspend its most advanced models for nearly three weeks — the design is a one-door regulator with a court in front of it: one agency, one court, three enumerated harms, a dollar and casualty floor. The D.C. Circuit upheld the Pentagon’s supply-chain-risk designation 2–1 on 25 Sep (en banc rehearing sought); whether the clause would reach a procurement exclusion is not knowable without the text.

Preemption, and a position change. Per Reuters, the draft would also override certain state laws, such as California’s. In June Anthropic wrote that “absent a strong federal law, states should retain full authority to legislate” — consistent only if this is the strong federal law. Charlie Bullock of the Institute for Law & AI called it “pretty reasonable” and “more than other frontier labs have done,” but “weaker” than the FRONTIER Act introduced in the House this summer.

Anthropic’s framing; the calendar. A spokesperson told Reuters the bill reflects its “Advanced AI Framework” and would give government the “ability to block or deter dangerous deployments of the most powerful models,” while requiring testing, transparency and independent evaluation. Commerce did not immediately respond. No sponsor is named, the text is unpublished, and Congress is out of session until after the 3 November midterms. The thresholds are the substance: a $900m cyber risk, or a serious but not “imminent” one, triggers nothing.

What to watch: a named sponsor and the text; a side-by-side against the FRONTIER Act; whether OpenAI or Google answer with drafts; California’s reaction to the preemption clause.

The policy move inside the price. What to watch: a CFPB or OCC statement on agent-initiated payments (Regulation E liability is the natural home); Q3 deposit-cost lines at the regionals reporting this week.

FMS batch as a lead:

An Anthropic research model solved the decades-old mystery of the NSA-supplied NIST curve seeds in under half an hour — they are lrand48 output seeded with 1997–98 Unix timestamps, which makes a backdoor less plausible, not more

AI & tech Lab safety Primary source New fact

The finding (primary, read). Anthropic says an internal research model, asked by an employee “without pointing at the clue” (the long-circulated story that the seeds hashed a humorous passphrase), recovered the origin of the P-192, P-224, P-256, P-384 and P-521 seeds — plus five ANSI X9.62 seeds — in under 30 minutes. They are not hashes at all: they are outputs of the C library’s lrand48 generator, seeded by Unix timestamps between May 1997 and April 1998. P-256’s seed is candidate 1,842 of a run started 16 Aug 1997, 01:52:34 UTC. Method: a bit-sliding scanner over 34 generator/slice combinations, a top-bit pattern with chance probability 1/5,810, brute force over 217 hidden bits, and LLL lattice reduction for the truncated outputs. Reproduction code is published; the authors include Nicholas Carlini and Steve Weis.

What it is not. Anthropic: “not a cryptographic break, new attack, or weakness” — P-256 secures most TLS and many wallets, and the result reduces the room for a deliberate weakness. Open items it lists: P-256 candidate 876 also gave a valid curve and was skipped; seed B-163 and the P-224/256/384/521 base points remain unexplained. The $36,864 bounty goes to the Internet Security Research Group.

Why it is a lab-safety item as much as a curiosity. The disclosure prints the model’s internal thinking, which includes: “Let me be skeptical: am I in some sort of simulated/test environment?” — evaluation awareness, the behaviour labs track because a model that suspects a test may behave differently, here surfacing unprompted on a real task (this brief’s reading, not Anthropic’s framing). And the capability is dual-use in plain sight: recovering PRNG state from published outputs is the same skill that breaks badly seeded keys. A lab that can do this in half an hour is also the lab pitching the bill in lead 3.
