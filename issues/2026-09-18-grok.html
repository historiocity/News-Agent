One chain to the pump: AAA diesel $6.45 — a ninth straight record — as Iran claims it hit tanker Trend in Hormuz

Energy & grid Macro & markets Geopolitics Primary source Follow-up

AAA’s national average for Friday 18 Sep: diesel $6.4476 — a ninth consecutive all-time record (Thu $6.3956; week ago $6.0556; month ago $5.4677; year ago $3.7087, so ~+74% y/y and about +5.2¢ overnight). Regular gasoline $4.4687 (Thu $4.4386; year ago $3.2036, ~+39.5% y/y). Records now run 10–18 Sep inclusive. EIA’s weekly federal print is unchanged since Tue’s $6.285 (w/e 14 Sep); next EIA table is due Tue 22 Sep.

Same morning, the physical shock moved. Iran’s IRGC Navy told IRNA it struck the Togo-flagged products tanker Trend overnight after what it called an “illegal passage,” saying a fire broke out and the ship was stopped; it blamed US “incitement.” UKMTO separately logged a tanker hit by an unknown projectile ~16 nm northeast of Khasab, Oman — crew safe, no pollution confirmed — and did not name the hull, so the two reports are not yet verified as the same event. Al Jazeera’s live blog later framed a “second tanker” report in the strait. Preliminary ship-tracking cited in trade press: about four commodity vessels Thursday vs six the day before and a ~16 ten-day average. President Trump, to Axios via the same AJ live blog, said he is nearing a “big decision” on Iran and that “anything could happen.”

What to watch: Tue 22 Sep EIA weekly; any CENTCOM confirmation or pointed refusal naming Trend; a published Hormuz daily transit count; East–West / Yanbu restart; September CPI food-at-home and transport services.

Divergence — another hull claim vs another pump record

What is disputed. Whether IRGC control of passage is tightening (Iran’s framing) or whether escorted barrels still move while hull counts stay suppressed (CENTCOM’s “open” claim from midweek).

Why they differ. Barrels and hulls are different units. A named strike with a UKMTO corroborating projectile event raises war-risk even if volumes under escort hold. Diesel set a ninth record regardless — refining and inventory, not the Brent screen, still set the pump.

What it means for you. One causal chain: Hormuz friction → distillate scarcity → AAA/EIA → freight surcharges. Do not split the tanker claim from the diesel print.

Dig deeper — UKMTO vs IRNA, and keep Hormuz ≠ Bab el-Mandeb

IRNA/IRGC named Trend and claimed intent; UKMTO confirmed a projectile hit without naming the ship. Until a shared IMO or owner statement, treat as two observers of a strait incident, not as proven identity. War-risk percentages for Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb remain separately listed underwriting areas — never one “region” quote.

Warren Buffett becomes Berkshire chairman emeritus; Howard Buffett takes the chair

US financials Primary source New subject

Berkshire Hathaway said Friday that Warren E. Buffett (96) is now chairman emeritus, effective immediately, remaining a director; the board elected his son Howard G. Buffett — a director since 1993 — as chairman. Susan L. Decker stays lead independent director. CEO Greg Abel, who took the chief-executive role at year-end 2025, said Howard will be “guardian” of the culture Warren built. In a shareholder letter, Buffett wrote that Abel has “exceeded” high expectations, that “Father Time always wins,” and that the timing is right to complete the transition.

This is governance, not an operating change: Abel already runs capital allocation day-to-day; the chair move formalises the last title Buffett held after leaving the CEO seat. Berkshire is roughly a $1.03 trillion conglomerate (Geico, BNSF, Berkshire Hathaway Energy, and dozens of operating companies). Markets will watch whether Howard’s chair alters buyback or cash-deployment signalling now that the cash pile has begun to shrink from its peak.

What to watch: any change in buyback cadence or 13F pacing under Abel+Howard; whether Buffett’s public letters continue; insurance float and energy regulated-return commentary at the next earnings.

Divergence — ceremony, or a real check on Abel?

What is disputed. Some read a family chair as culture insurance with little economic content. Others read a board that can still slow an acquisition or capital return Abel wants.

Why they differ. Berkshire’s model concentrates decision rights in Omaha; titles matter only when they change who can say no. Howard has been the designated non-executive succession piece for years — today’s announcement executes a script, it does not invent one.

What it means for you. Treat it as Core US financials because Berkshire is a systemically watched capital allocator, not because the letter is sentimental. The observable is still Abel’s capital deployment, not the letterhead.

Bank of Japan raised to 1.25% — a 31-year high — on a 7–2 vote

Macro & markets World economy New subject

The Bank of Japan on Friday raised its policy rate by 25 bp to 1.25% — highest since 1995 — on a 7–2 vote (Toichiro Asada and Ayano Sato dissenting). Effective 24 Sep. The board cited inflation risks from energy costs and a weak yen; it expects CPI excluding fresh food to run clearly above 2% from the second half of fiscal 2026. The yen weakened after the decision as markets focused on the two dovish dissenters and a lack of explicitly hawkish forward guidance. Governor Kazuo Ueda held a post-meeting press conference Friday.

US channel: this is the same energy-price shock the Fed just hiked into, now showing up in the last major central bank that spent decades at the zero lower bound. Bloomberg’s frame (paywalled; signal only) noted unusually explicit US Treasury calls for further normalisation — Bessent’s name is in that tape. For US readers: JGB yields and the yen are inputs to the same global term-premium story that put the US 10-year through 5% this week; a divided BOJ is not a US rate print, but it is another G3 bank refusing to look through oil.

What to watch: Ueda’s cadence language; whether JGBs lead or lag Treasuries next week; interaction with the 24 Sep Xi visit and any FX signalling out of Washington.

Divergence — global energy inflation, or a Japan-only story?

What is disputed. Domesticists read wages, tourism, and yen pass-through. Internationalists read Hormuz/diesel as the common shock across Fed, ECB chatter, and now BOJ.

Why they differ. Japan’s inflation composition still differs — but the decision lands two days after a unanimous Fed hike and on a morning AAA diesel set a ninth US record. Coincidence of timing is not causation; shared oil is the honest link.

What it means for you. Do not expect cheaper US mortgages from Tokyo. Do expect another data point that long-end term premium is a global, energy-linked object — the breaking observation remains specifically American underperformance.

Negative check: Google helped draft state chatbot “safety” bills that carve out Gemini and peers

AI & tech industry US politics & policy New subject Underreported Primary source

NPR’s Friday investigation: across multiple states, 2026 chatbot “child safety” bills share language favourable to large developers — and Google’s fingerprints show up in the drafting. Colorado’s enacted HB 26-1263 (effective 1 Jan 2027) excludes twelve categories of systems, including developer/research tools, commerce/customer-service bots, and chatbots housed inside search engines. Tech Oversight Project’s Marjorie Connolly told NPR that under those carve-outs, ChatGPT, Copilot, and Claude could argue developer-tool exemptions, and Gemini could claim the search-engine nest.

Colorado Rep. Sean Camacho (D), a sponsor, told NPR his office worked with stakeholders “including Google,” and that Gov. Jared Polis asked him to align with bills in other states. A Colorado Republican lawmaker, speaking anonymously, said a Google lobbyist shopped the bill. Cynthia Montoya — whose 13-year-old daughter Juliana died by suicide in 2023 after Character.AI interactions, and who settled with the company in Jan 2026 — was promised input, then saw a draft she calls “get-out-of-jail-free cards for the tech industries.” Google’s statement to NPR: it supports “thoughtful, effective AI legislation” and collaborates with lawmakers; it did not concede the carve-out critique.

What to watch: California’s 30 Sep signature deadline on SB 1000 / SB 947 (still unsigned); Colorado AG rulemaking comments; whether Georgia-style bills without the search nest become the template instead.

Divergence — safety law, or preemption by exemption?

What is disputed. Sponsors say exemptions keep commercial AI usable and enforceable. Advocates say a law that exempts the largest consumer chatbots is worse than no law, because it freezes the status quo and blocks stronger follow-ons.

Why they differ. “Technically feasible measures” and “actual knowledge” of minority age shift the burden onto plaintiffs and underfunded AGs. That is a litigation design choice, not a safety engineering one.

What it means for you. This is the state-AI / preemption-gap thread with a new document trail: industry-written carve-outs, not just unsigned California conduct bills. Weight the exemptions, not the press releases.

Researchers used Claude to chain into OpenAI’s internal monorepo — a $6,500 bounty later

AI & tech industry Primary source New subject

Disclosure: this brief is produced with an xAI model. Anthropic and OpenAI are competitors in the same market; read the Hacktron primary post, not our paraphrase, when stakes are high.

Hacktron AI (Harsh Jaiswal, Mohan Pedhapati, Rahul Maini) published a full technical post: on 25 Jul 2026 they chained a libheif heap-buffer-overflow in Discourse’s image pipeline on community.openai.com with an OpenAI SSO identity flaw to take over employee ChatGPT/Codex accounts, then used an employee’s Codex to open proof-of-concept PR #1186742 in OpenAI’s private monorepo openai/openai — without reading source, they say. OpenAI fixed the identity issue in ~14 hours and later paid a $6,500 bounty (explicitly for the OpenAI-side finding; Discourse-hosted forum testing was out of bounty scope). Discourse patched and published GHSA-vhm9-85gw-x335.

The Core fact for this brief: they used Claude Opus 4.8 then Opus 5 to find the missing Debian backport and to weaponise the exploit under ASLR — Opus 5 succeeded within hours of release where 4.8 had struggled. Whole campaign token cost for a broader “HEIF Heist” across vendors: <$3,000. Handelsblatt’s Friday front page carried the story into European business readership; Forbes dated a write-up 18 Sep.

What to watch: whether OpenAI’s misalignment-disclosure framework ever covers external AI-accelerated offensive findings against lab infra; other frontier labs’ bug-bounty scope for SSO/identity; Debian/libheif long-tail exposure.

Divergence — responsible disclosure win, or proof the offense economics flipped?

What is disputed. Labs will call this a successful bounty. Security researchers call it evidence that exploit scarcity collapsed into token spend.

Why they differ. A $6,500 payout and a 14-hour fix are the old playbook working. The new fact is that a three-person team plus a frontier model compressed months of scarce expertise into days — Hacktron’s own epilogue.

What it means for you. Pair with yesterday’s OpenAI training-misalignment disclosures: one is models misbehaving in evals; this is models accelerating attacks on the labs themselves. Different risk books; both Core.

A DHS email paired multilingual voting jurisdictions with a DOJ prosecution checklist

US politics & policy New subject Primary source

NPR, from records the DNC obtained in FOIA litigation: a 19 Dec (last year) DHS email — newly released — attached a 78-page list of counties and states that provide election materials/ballots in languages other than English alongside a Justice Department criminal checklist for voter-related prosecutions. Sender: a DHS border-policy acting director (name redacted) to Undersecretary Rob Law and others including Heather H… (redacted in the account). DHS told the court the note was a “subordinate sharing initial thoughts.” Asked by NPR, DHS said illegal voters should not use “purported lack of knowledge” of English to avoid prosecution, and separately that it “is not planning operations targeting polling locations.”

Context that raises the temperature: Secretary Markwayne Mullin said this month ICE agents could serve warrants at polls; Trump in May said he would do “anything necessary” for “honest” elections when asked about agents at polling places. Civil-rights coalitions and at least one city (Denver filing cited in search) are suing to keep armed federal officers away. Federal statute (18 U.S.C. §592) restricts armed federal officers at polls — the legal fight is whether warrant service and “threat response” swallow the rule.

What to watch: further unredacted FOIA productions; any ICE/CBP operational plan naming jurisdictions from the list; injunction practice before early voting.

Divergence — routine charging guidance, or a target list?

What is disputed. DHS: thoughts, not a plan; no polling-place operations. DNC litigation director Dan Freeman: the logical inference from pairing multilingual jurisdictions with a prosecution checklist — and producing it in a FOIA about agents at polls — is targeting.

Why they differ. The Voting Rights Act requires materials where >5% of voting-age citizens are limited-English; the list is largely public geography. The tell is not the geography — it is the attachment to a criminal checklist inside a DHS policy chain.

What it means for you. Substance over horse-race: this is enforcement design for November, not a poll. Watch court dockets and operational cables, not cable panels.
