Trump, Xi and Putin sat at one table for 80 minutes. Beijing and Moscow call the result a “peace declaration”; Washington has not said what it signed

Geopolitics / US policy Continuing · Shenzhen trilateral Independent corroboration

The first three-way meeting of American, Chinese and Russian leaders took place on Thursday at the Kylin Villa in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of APEC. It lasted about an hour and twenty minutes, according to DW and the New York Post. Trump left directly for the airport around 5am ET and, Fox News reported, was not expected to speak publicly before flying home.

In front of cameras, Xi said great powers at peace keep the world secure; Trump said “we are really making history.” Xinhua quoted Xi calling for nuclear weapons to be comprehensively banned and destroyed. NPR’s account is that the three came with different agendas: Trump on nuclear reductions and Iran, Putin on ending the Ukraine war on Moscow’s terms, Xi on avoiding both.

Afterwards, Chinese state media and the Kremlin said, as relayed by AFP and Reuters, that the three had adopted a joint “peace declaration”. Finland’s Yle summarised its published text: a pledge to preserve strategic stability, keep talking about nuclear security and non-proliferation, reform the UN, and a line that the International Criminal Court and similar bodies must not exceed their jurisdiction. Le Figaro and Al Jazeera also report a commitment to stable energy and oil-product supplies.

What it means for you: the oil-supply language matters more for pump prices than the nuclear language does, and only if it is followed by Russian or Chinese action. What to watch: whether the White House publishes the declaration’s text or describes it differently.

Divergence — was there an agreement, or just a photograph?

What is disputed. A significance dispute with a facts edge. Chinese and Russian state outlets describe an adopted joint declaration and Xi called the summit “positive, constructive and fruitful.” US outlets in this run (Fox News, NPR) describe no major breakthrough; NPR says the key deliverable was arguably the image itself.

Why they differ. Timing first: US reports were filed as Trump left, before the joint text circulated, and the White House has not briefed. Interest second: Beijing hosted and wants the summit to look productive; Moscow wants to be seen at the top table. US domestic outlets are weighing it against what Trump said he wanted, which was nuclear reductions.

What it means for you. Both are compatible: a declaration exists, and on this run’s reading it contains commitments to keep talking, not numbers or deadlines. Treat it as atmospherics until a US official confirms the text. A follow-on date for nuclear talks, or a named oil-supply measure, would settle it.

Dig deeper — the ICC line

The clause that the ICC must not exceed its jurisdiction is the one item with a clear beneficiary: Putin is under an ICC arrest warrant. The US is not an ICC member and has sanctioned its officials before, so the line costs Washington little, but it is the first time a US president has put his name to it alongside Moscow, if the text Yle summarised is final.

Anthropic priced at $83 and raised $81.3bn, the largest IPO ever; it starts trading on Nasdaq today as “ANT”

AI & tech / US financials Continuing · Anthropic IPO Primary source

Anthropic said late on Wednesday that it and its seven co-founders sold about 980 million shares at $83, above the $70–$78 range it marketed last week, when it planned 825 million shares. That raises $81.3bn, ahead of SpaceX’s initial $75bn in June ($85.7bn after its overallotment). CNBC puts the fully diluted value at about $1.5 trillion.

The company’s own filing shows why this is a test of the AI trade rather than a victory lap: $16.3bn of revenue in the first half of 2026, up nearly 14-fold, against a net loss of $80.7bn over the same six months, ABC reported. Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and AMD have agreed to invest a further $26bn at the IPO price, and Anthropic is a large buyer of their chips and cloud capacity.

For ordinary savers the route in is the index: a Nasdaq rule change in May allows “fast entry” to the Nasdaq-100 for some very large listings, ABC noted, so index funds in 401(k)s are likely to hold the stock soon.

Divergence — how much did Anthropic raise: $81.3bn or $107.3bn?

What is disputed. A facts dispute that is really a definitions dispute. CNBC, ABC and Al Jazeera report $81.3bn. Bloomberg’s headline and CoinDesk report $107.3bn.

Why they differ. The larger figure adds the $26bn private placement to the tech companies to the public offering. Both numbers come from the same company release; they count different things. The circularity is worth noting: much of that $26bn comes from firms that sell Anthropic its compute.

What it means for you. For record comparisons with SpaceX, $81.3bn is the like-for-like number. For how much cash Anthropic has to spend, $107.3bn is closer. Nothing further will settle it; just check which a headline is using.

Sources: CNBC · ABC News · Al Jazeera · Bloomberg (headline)

The $350bn defence reconciliation bill is dead, Senate Republicans lack 50 votes for a smaller one, and Golden Dome is getting about half its ask

US debt & fiscal / US policy New Independent corroboration

The outgoing House Armed Services chairman, Mike Rogers, said on Wednesday that a $350bn reconciliation bill is “not going to happen,” though a skinnied-down version might, to cover the hole Venezuela and Iran operations have made in the defence budget, Breaking Defense reported. Semafor reports Senate Republicans are at least temporarily abandoning a party-line defence bill because they do not have 50 votes, and are turning to negotiations with Democrats.

The deadlines are tight: the continuing resolution funding the Pentagon runs out on 11 December, and Democrats take both chambers on 3 January. Senator Kevin Cramer told Semafor that without 50 votes for a budget resolution, December leaves no time for two vote-a-ramas.

Meanwhile, Senate appropriators released a nearly $1.1 trillion fiscal 2027 defence draft on Monday. Defense One reports it puts about $9.4bn toward Golden Dome, against a $17.5bn request that the administration had placed almost entirely in reconciliation, and Breaking Defense says it pays for that and for drones and munitions with cuts to shipbuilding, F-35 purchases and research.

Divergence — did appropriators back Golden Dome or cut it?

What is disputed. A significance dispute over the same number. Breaking Defense frames the Senate draft as putting billions behind Golden Dome. Defense One frames it as slashing the request roughly in half.

Why they differ. Both are true from different baselines. Measured against the base budget, where only about $398m was requested, $9.4bn is a large increase. Measured against the $17.5bn total ask, most of which sat in a reconciliation bill now not expected to pass, it is a cut. Breaking Defense covers programmes and industry; Defense One here leans on budget analysts.

What it means for you. The more stable money is the $9.4bn in the regular bill, if it passes. With reconciliation fading, that is the realistic ceiling for now. A new continuing resolution on 11 December that carries over old levels would mean neither number arrives.

Sources: Breaking Defense (Rogers) · Breaking Defense ($1.1tn) · Defense One · Semafor

CENTCOM extends the Iran blockade to ship-to-ship transfers, the relay that has kept Gulf oil moving, while tankers keep getting hit near Hormuz

Oil & chokepoints Continuing · Gulf oil Primary source

US Central Command said in a Wednesday advisory that any ship receiving cargo by ship-to-ship transfer from a blockaded vessel carrying Iranian-loaded cargo may be treated as evading the blockade, even if it never enters Iranian waters. Such ships may be stopped, boarded, diverted or captured, the advisory says. Neutral transit through Hormuz to non-Iranian destinations is not affected, according to the same NAVCENT update. The blockade was reimposed on 14 July after a draft understanding with Tehran collapsed, Reuters (via gCaptain) noted.

The attacks continue. UKMTO logged two tanker attacks in the strait on Monday, both causing engine-room fires, and one on Sunday. Al Jazeera’s live page on Thursday reports five tankers ablaze after hitting naval mines in and around Hormuz; the IRGC Navy claims two were being escorted by US jets on an unauthorised route.

Prices have not jumped. OilPrice puts Brent at about $92 and WTI at about $83, with forecasters warning of a glut as OPEC+ output rises and Russian exports hold. Vortexa said Iranian restrictions have not yet reduced the volume moving through the Gulf shuttle relay, because exporters added another stage of ship-to-ship transfers.

Divergence — is the Gulf supply squeeze easing or tightening?

What is disputed. A significance dispute. Vortexa and the oversupply forecasters say flows are holding and the market is heading for a glut. The incident stream from UKMTO and Al Jazeera, plus the CENTCOM advisory, points the other way.

Why they differ. Vantage: Vortexa measures barrels moved, which lag risk; UKMTO and wire reports measure incidents, which are noisy and partly sourced to the IRGC, a combatant. Timing: the CENTCOM advisory is one day old and Vortexa’s comment predates it. The advisory targets Iranian-origin cargo, not the Gulf states’ relay, but both use the same hulls, crews and transfer sites.

What it means for you. Today’s evidence supports flows holding. What would change that is an actual US interdiction of a ship-to-ship transfer, or insurers pulling cover for relay tankers. Until then, the glut reading carries more weight for pump prices.

Sources: gCaptain / Reuters (CENTCOM advisory) · gCaptain (UKMTO) · Al Jazeera live · OilPrice

The NSA outage reached a Georgia hub for Middle East intelligence; a second critical PHP patch lands today

AI & tech / national security Continuing · outages Independent corroboration

The National Security Agency’s IT disruption, now two days long, also hit a Georgia facility that supports signals intelligence and cyber operations focused on the Middle East, where the US is at war with Iran, four sources told CNN. Officials stressed that the most sensitive collection systems are working, with back-ups in place, and that some old, compartmented networks were untouched.

CBS, citing multiple sources, ties the NSA outage to urgent patching for PHP2Shell, a pair of critical PHP flaws found with AI help and the subject of an FBI, NSA and CISA warning on 21 October. DNI Jay Clayton made the same link on Fox Business. The Register reports the PHP Foundation will ship another critical security release today between 9:00 and 9:30am ET, including a remote-code-execution flaw.

On the FAA, CBS reports that L3Harris’s network centre in Melbourne, Florida took several hours to pinpoint the El Dorado, Arkansas router site during Friday’s ground stop. Transportation Secretary Duffy said L3Harris “didn’t identify and isolate.” Microsoft’s Satya Nadella blamed Outlook’s outage on a latent bug and warned that much critical infrastructure is “unpatchable.”

FBI Director Kash Patel skipped Wednesday’s House threat hearing on the outages and sent his deputy, after being billed the night before as an honoured guest at a $50,000-a-head Mar-a-Lago fundraiser, CNBC reported.

Divergence — one common cause, or several?

What is disputed. A facts dispute about cause. Clayton’s framing, reported by the NY Post and CBS, is a common cause: institutions updating legacy systems in a rush. The specific accounts differ: CBS ties the NSA to PHP2Shell; the FAA failure involved a new router and a contractor’s fault-finding; Nadella describes a latent Microsoft bug.

Why they differ. Clayton speaks for the government as a whole and has a reason to offer a single reassuring explanation. Reporters with sources inside each incident see different mechanisms. The mundane reading is that a heavy, AI-accelerated patch cycle raised the odds of failures that each had their own trigger.

What it means for you. Believe “no attack”: no account in this run contradicts it. Do not take “one cause” literally. Today’s PHP release is a live test: if more outages follow this week, the patch-cycle explanation strengthens.

Sources: CNN · CBS (NSA) · CBS (L3Harris) · The Register · TechCrunch · CNBC (Patel) · NY Post (Clayton)

A Russian man who crossed from Mexico is charged with spying on Palantir staff in Palo Alto because of the company’s work for Ukraine

National security / AI & tech New Independent corroboration

Federal prosecutors in San Francisco have charged Igor D’iu with acting as an agent of a foreign government, interstate stalking and possessing falsified identity documents, after what they describe as a covert operation in Palo Alto. The charging papers do not name the company; CNN (two sources), the BBC and ABC identify it as Palantir, whose software Ukraine uses for targeting and war-crimes investigations.

According to the Justice Department, D’iu travelled from Istanbul to Mexico in June, entered the US unlawfully, and took instructions, equipment and money from co-conspirators over an encrypted app. He believed he was working for “the Motherland,” court documents say, and was found with two Russian passports. US Attorney Craig Missakian said the case describes a state-sponsored operation to harass employees of an American defence company.

Divergence — harassment campaign or a way into Palantir’s systems?

What is disputed. A significance dispute. The public charges and the US Attorney describe stalking and harassing employees. Two sources told CNN the aim was to “get into the company’s systems” rather than target individuals.

Why they differ. Prosecutors charge what they can prove in court; intent to penetrate networks may sit in sealed material or be unproven. CNN’s sources are anonymous and familiar with the case, which gives access but cannot be checked.

What it means for you. The charged conduct is the floor. Treat the systems-access aim as plausible but unconfirmed. A superseding indictment or a detention-hearing filing describing digital targeting would settle it.

Sources: CNN · BBC · ABC News · CBS News

America’s biggest data centres already use 1.8% of its electricity, and the IEA says global data-centre demand could double by 2030

AI & tech / power New Independent corroboration

A Harvard study published this week in Nature Sustainability found that 403 hyperscale data centres consumed 82 terawatt-hours between May 2024 and April 2025, 1.8% of US electricity, roughly Missouri’s use. The lead author told CBS the figure likely understates today’s load: the count of large facilities has more than doubled since, with about 2,000 more planned.

Separately, the International Energy Agency warned that global data-centre use could reach about 1,000 TWh a year by 2030 under its stated-policies scenario, roughly double, and that grid capacity, supply chains and local opposition could delay projects, CNBC reported. In several regions, including Texas and Virginia, capacity waiting to connect already exceeds current peak demand.

What it means for you: the cost of new lines and transformers lands in utility rates; this is the mechanism behind the data-centre backlash already visible in the midterms.

Sources: CBS News (Harvard study) · CNBC (IEA)

Denmark ratifies the Greenland security deal 109–0, clearing the way for more US bases without a Senate vote

Geopolitics / US policy New Independent corroboration

All 109 members of the Folketing present voted on Thursday for the security agreement the US, Denmark and Greenland signed in September, without debate or abstention, Al Jazeera and CBS report. Greenland’s parliament passed it earlier this month. CBS says the deal paves the way for more US bases on the island and eases tensions over Trump’s repeated calls to take control of it.

It is an executive agreement, not a treaty, so the Senate does not vote. It takes effect once Denmark and Greenland notify the US by diplomatic note that their parliaments have finished.

Sources: CBS News · Al Jazeera

More than a foot of rain on Florida’s Atlantic coast, a record St. Johns River at Cocoa, and two more days of flood threat

Florida & Tampa Bay New Independent corroboration

Plantation has recorded 16.4 inches over two days, Hollywood 13.8 and Fort Lauderdale 12.2, ABC reports. In Hollywood more than a foot fell in hours, the mayor declared an emergency, parts of I-95 and the Turnpike closed, and Temple Sinai’s roof collapsed, CNN reported. The St. Johns River near Cocoa reached a record 17.8 feet, cutting off neighbourhoods in Cocoa and Rockledge.

More rain is due Thursday and Friday: a Level 3 of 4 flood threat on the Space Coast, extending to Orlando on Friday, ABC says. CNN says the system could take on tropical characteristics and snarl early Thanksgiving travel up the East Coast. Tampa Bay is outside the threat area; CBS Miami reports Hollywood’s water receding.

Sources: ABC News · CNN · CBS Miami live
