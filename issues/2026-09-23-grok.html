Negative check: CMS freezes new ACA agent and broker registrations through 1 February

In today’s Federal Register, HHS/CMS publishes an interim final rule with comment (CMS-9872-IFC; RIN 0938-AW26; FR Doc. 2026-19493) that codifies authority at 45 CFR §155.220(o) to impose temporary moratoria on certain agent and broker registrations with the Federally-facilitated Exchanges — and, in the same document, imposes one immediately.

Effective 22 September 2026, CMS pauses registration of agents and brokers that do not have Plan Year 2026 Exchange agreements and want Plan Year 2027 access. The pause runs until 1 February 2027 unless lifted earlier or extended by further FR notice. It does not cover web-brokers, does not hit State-based Exchanges that run their own platforms, and does not block reinstatement after a reversed termination.

The load-bearing numbers in the preamble: from 2023–2025 CMS received over 624,000 consumer complaints of unauthorized enrollments or plan switches confirmed by issuer review — roughly 300,000 in 2025 alone. Newly registered Plan Year 2026 agents were ~11% of agents with active enrollments but ~30% of the 569 Notices of Intent to Terminate issued in July–August 2026. CMS estimates the temporary pause prevents improper APTC outlays in a modeled range of roughly $48 million to $877 million annually and transfers ~$71–98 million in commission revenue toward already-registered brokers. Comments close 21 November 2026. OIRA tagged the action significant/major under E.O. 12866 and the CRA; HHS waived prior notice-and-comment and the ordinary delayed effective date on good-cause grounds tied to Open Enrollment starting 1 November.

What to watch: whether state AGs or broker trade groups sue the IFC; whether the enhanced Login.gov/ID.me identity-proofing and SSN/immigration verification CMS describes actually land before OE; any early lift or extension of the February 1 date.

Divergence — program integrity vs gatekeeping

What is disputed. Whether a prospective pause on new entrants is a tailored response to measured over-representation of newly registered brokers in unauthorized-enrollment enforcement, or a structural preference for incumbents that shifts ~$70–100m in commissions.

Why they differ. CMS’s own ratios (11% of agents → 30% of NOITs; 2.6–2.8× higher rates of missing SSNs and unresolved citizenship/income DMIs) are the case for targeting new entrants. The quantified commission transfer and the estimated 19,000 registrations that would otherwise occur during the window are the case for calling it a market-structure intervention.

What it means for you. Core US policy with a fiscal channel (APTC). Came from the standing FR RULE/PRORULE sweep — not a beat tip and not on yesterday’s Grok menu.

Dig deeper — why good-cause mattered here

The preamble is unusually explicit that a proposed rule would have been self-defeating: registration takes under 30 days, Open Enrollment is six weeks away, and announcing a forthcoming pause would accelerate the very registrations the pause is meant to stop. That is a stronger good-cause story than most IFCs manage — and it is also the legal vulnerability plaintiffs will target first. Severability language is in the document; if a court voids the concurrent moratorium notice but leaves §155.220(o) standing, CMS keeps the toolkit for later.

Commerce publishes the zero-tariff specialty-pharma list six days before Section 232 bites

Also in today’s Federal Register: BIS notice (Docket 260918-0006; RIN 0694-XC154; FR Doc. 2026-19498) implementing clause 3(d) of Presidential Proclamation 11020 (2 April 2026) — the Section 232 pharmaceutical action. It defines which specialty products get an ad valorem tariff of zero: orphan-only indications, nuclear medicines, plasma-derived therapies, fertility drugs, cell and gene therapies, antibody-drug conjugates, CBRN medical countermeasures, and animal-health products — if they are products of a listed jurisdiction with a trade-and-security framework agreement, or if Commerce (with USTR and HHS) finds an urgent US health need.

Eligible jurisdictions named today: Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Ecuador, El Salvador, European Union, Guatemala, India, Indonesia, Japan, Jordan, Malaysia, North Macedonia, Republic of Korea, Switzerland and Liechtenstein, Taiwan, Thailand, United Kingdom, Vietnam. Urgent-need petitions open immediately (email to BIS). HTSUS technical corrections take effect for entries on/after 12:01 a.m. ET 29 September 2026 — the date the 100% rate expands beyond the Annex III early list. Generics remain outside the Section 232 patented-pharma tariff for now.

What to watch: first urgent-need approvals before the 29 Sep cliff; whether onshoring-agreement companies already on preferential tracks collide with the specialty list; any EU/India industry pushback on definitions.

Divergence — carve-out vs industrial policy

What is disputed. Whether zero-rating orphan/gene/cell therapies is a patient-access safety valve, or the hole that lets the 100% tariff’s pressure escape.

Why they differ. The proclamation always contemplated clause 3(d); today’s notice is the operating manual, not a repeal. The timing — six days before the broad effective date — is why it leads.

What it means for you. Trade mechanism and market effect, per settled preference. FR primary; not on yesterday’s menu.

Kalshi asks to list perpetual security futures on single-name equities

SEC notice (Release No. 34-106422; File No. SR-KALSHIEX-2026-02; FR Doc. 2026-19409), dated 18 Sep and published 23 Sep: KalshiEX LLC filed under Exchange Act §19(b)(7) to adopt Rulebook Chapter 14 authorizing Perpetual SFPs — cash-settled security futures with no pre-specified expiry that keep perpetual-style funding payments pulling the contract toward the underlying NMS equity. Same Federal Register day carries companion security-futures listing-standard filings from Coinbase Derivatives and Bitnomial.

Kalshi’s proposed screens are stricter than Staff Legal Bulletin 15 samples: initial underlying market cap ≥$100bn, six-month ADTV ≥$450m (or $1bn if listed <6 months), estimated deliverable supply >20m shares; customer margin 15.50% (above the joint SEC/CFTC 15% floor); position limits 50,000 standard contract equivalents (25,000 in the last three days before any forced final settlement). CFTC approval of the DCM rule change is still pending in the notice. Clearing via Kalshi Klear.

What to watch: CFTC action; first Approved Securities Table names; whether the funding mechanism survives contact with Reg SHO / manipulation concerns on megacap names.

Divergence — crypto plumbing into equity risk

What is disputed. Whether perpetual funding on single-name equities is a legitimate risk-transfer tool, or a supervised path for 24/5 leveraged equity exposure outside traditional futures months.

Why they differ. Kalshi already has CFTC-approved bitcoin perpetuals; the filing’s claim is that “future delivery” does not require a fixed date. Equity-market structure veterans will read the $100bn/ADTV screens as the real story — only megacaps qualify at launch.

What it means for you. Core financials with a tech channel. Three venues moving the same day is the tell.

New York leads eight AGs against $1.4bn offshore-wind buyouts paid from the Judgment Fund

On 22 Sep (Climate Week), NY AG Letitia James and Governor Kathy Hochul announced two federal lawsuits with Connecticut, Delaware, Maine, Massachusetts, New Jersey, Rhode Island and Vermont. The targets: Interior deals that cancel offshore-wind leases in exchange for Judgment Fund payments — $765m to Bluepoint Wind (NY lease; company redirected toward an LNG facility and barred from future US offshore wind) and $653m to Invenergy (three leases including one off NY; money redirected to gas plants in IN/WI/IA/KS/MO and western geothermal). Combined ~$1.4bn. California filed a concurrent suit on a separate Invenergy California lease.

State framing: the Judgment Fund is for legitimate claims against the United States; these agreements allegedly resolve no actual or imminent litigation, violate the APA, NEPA, OCSLA and Judgment Fund statutes, and remove >8 GW that planners counted on while NY electricity demand is projected +8% by 2030 / +24% by 2040 (data centres called out). Relief sought: void the deals and lease cancellations.

What to watch: TRO/preliminary-injunction posture; whether DOE/DOI defend the Judgment Fund theory; any further buyout announcements during the litigation.

Divergence — settlement tool vs spending end-run

What is disputed. Whether paying lessees to exit is a lawful way to unwind prior leasing, or an unlawful use of a litigation fund to buy a policy outcome courts had already constrained.

Why they differ. Administration: no future for offshore wind; pay-to-exit avoids years of contested permitting. States: after winning earlier injunctions, the buyouts are the workaround.

What it means for you. Energy/grid with a fiscal channel — and a data-centre demand hook on a Core-adjacent beat.

Shield of the Americas: fifteen governments name 24 cartels for asset freezes and visa bans

State Department released a 22 Sep joint statement (Office of the Spokesman) from the United States plus Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guyana, Honduras, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, and Trinidad and Tobago. Under the Shield of the Americas’ security pillar, the governments “declare [their] intention” to impose — as applicable under domestic law — asset freezes, immigration/visa restrictions, and criminal liability for knowing material support, focused on a published list of 24 organizations (MS-13, Tren de Aragua, CJNG, Sinaloa, Cartel de Los Soles, PCC, Comando Vermelho, ELN, FARC dissidents, Shining Path, and others).

They also intend to co-sign a request for an OAS Permanent Council meeting of consultation toward an Organ of Consultation under Articles 6 and 13 of the Rio Treaty. Non-parties to the Rio Treaty in the Shield say they will support to the extent their law allows. Al Jazeera/France 24 same-morning wraps cast Trump as urging the coalition to treat the groups as common regional threats.

What to watch: actual designation/sanctions packages (intention ≠ order); whether Mexico participates or objects; Rio Treaty Organ of Consultation scheduling.

Divergence — coalition theatre vs binding instruments

What is disputed. Whether a fifteen-government “intention to impose” list is the start of coordinated hemispheric sanctions, or a UNGA-week communiqué that still requires each capital’s domestic machinery.

Why they differ. The list and the Rio Treaty pathway are concrete. “As applicable and in accordance with … domestic legal obligations” is the escape hatch.

What it means for you. US policy substance with a sanctions channel — not a foreign election story.

Sources: State.gov joint statement (primary) · Al Jazeera · France 24 live wrap
